p11: We, the people of Kenya—
p11: ACKNOWLEDGING the supremacy of the Almighty God of all
p11: creation:
p11: HONOURING those who heroically struggled to bring freedom
p11: and justice to our land:
p11: PROUD of our ethnic, cultural and religious diversity, and
p11: determined to live in peace and unity as one indivisible sovereign
p11: nation:
p11: RESPECTFUL of the environment, which is our heritage, and
p11: determined to sustain it for the benefit of future generations:
p11: COMMITTED to nurturing and protecting the well-being of the
p11: individual, the family, communities and the nation:
p11: RECOGNISING the aspirations of all Kenyans for a government
p11: based on the essential values of human rights, equality, freedom,
p11: democracy, social justice and the rule of law:
p11: EXERCISING our sovereign and inalienable right to determine
p11: the form of governance of our country and having participated fully in
p11: the making of this Constitution:
p11: ADOPT, ENACT and give this Constitution to ourselves and to
p11: our future generations.

p12: THE CONSTITUTION OF KENYA
p12: CHAPTER ONE—SOVEREIGNTY OF THE PEOPLE AND
p12: SUPREMACY OF THIS CONSTITUTION
p12: Sovereignty of the people.
p12: 1. (1) All sovereign power belongs to the people of Kenya and
p12: shall be exercised only in accordance with this Constitution.
p12: (2) The people may exercise their sovereign power either directly
p12: or through their democratically elected representatives.
p12: (3) Sovereign power under this Constitution is delegated to the
p12: following State organs, which shall perform their functions in
p12: accordance with this Constitution—
p12: (a) Parliament and the legislative assemblies in the county
p12: governments;
p12: (b) the national executive and the executive structures in the
p12: county governments; and
p12: (c) the Judiciary and independent tribunals.
p12: (4) The sovereign power of the people is exercised at—
p12: (a) the national level; and
p12: (b) the county level.
p12: Supremacy of this Constitution.
p12: 2. (1) This Constitution is the supreme law of the Republic and
p12: binds all persons and all State organs at both levels of government.
p12: (2) No person may claim or exercise State authority except as
p12: authorised under this Constitution.
p12: (3) The validity or legality of this Constitution is not subject to
p12: challenge by or before any court or other State organ.
p12: (4) Any law, including customary law, that is inconsistent with this
p12: Constitution is void to the extent of the inconsistency, and any act or
p12: omission in contravention of this Constitution is invalid.
p12: (5) The general rules of international law shall form part of the
p12: law of Kenya.
p13: (6) Any treaty or convention ratified by Kenya shall form part of
p13: the law of Kenya under this Constitution.
p13: Defence of this Constitution.
p13: 3. (1) Every person has an obligation to respect, uphold and
p13: defend this Constitution.
p13: (2) Any attempt to establish a government otherwise than in
p13: compliance with this Constitution is unlawful.
p13: CHAPTER TWO—THE REPUBLIC
p13: Declaration of the Republic.
p13: 4. (1) Kenya is a sovereign Republic.
p13: (2) The Republic of Kenya shall be a multi-party democratic State
p13: founded on the national values and principles of governance referred
p13: to in Article 10.
p13: Territory of Kenya.
p13: 5. Kenya consists of the territory and territorial waters comprising
p13: Kenya on the effective date, and any additional territory and territorial
p13: waters as defined by an Act of Parliament.
p13: Devolution and access to services.
p13: 6. (1) The territory of Kenya is divided into the counties specified
p13: in the First Schedule.
p13: (2) The governments at the national and county levels are distinct
p13: and inter-dependent and shall conduct their mutual relations on the
p13: basis of consultation and cooperation.
p13: (3) A national State organ shall ensure reasonable access to its
p13: services in all parts of the Republic, so far as it is appropriate to do so
p13: having regard to the nature of the service.
p13: National, official and other languages.
p13: 7. (1) The national language of the Republic is Kiswahili.
p13: (2) The official languages of the Republic are Kiswahili and
p13: English.
p13: (3) The State shall—
p13: (a) promote and protect the diversity of language of the people of
p13: Kenya; and
p14: (b) promote the development and use of indigenous languages,
p14: Kenyan Sign language, Braille and other communication
p14: formats and technologies accessible to persons with
p14: disabilities.
p14: State and Religion.
p14: 8. There shall be no State religion.
p14: National symbols and national days.
p14: 9. (1) The national symbols of the Republic are—
p14: (a) the national flag;
p14: (b) the national anthem;
p14: (c) the coat of arms; and
p14: (d) the public seal.
p14: (2) The national symbols are as set out in the Second Schedule.
p14: (3) The national days are—
p14: (a) Madaraka Day, to be observed on 1st June;
p14: (b) Mashujaa Day, to be observed on 20th October; and
p14: (c) Jamhuri Day, to be observed on 12th December.
p14: (4) A national day shall be a public holiday.
p14: (5) Parliament may enact legislation prescribing other public
p14: holidays, and providing for observance of public holidays.
p14: National values and principles of governance.
p14: 10. (1) The national values and principles of governance in this
p14: Article bind all State organs, State officers, public officers and all
p14: persons whenever any of them—
p14: (a) applies or interprets this Constitution;
p14: (b) enacts, applies or interprets any law; or
p14: (c) makes or implements public policy decisions.
p14: (2) The national values and principles of governance include—
p15: (a) patriotism, national unity, sharing and devolution of power, the
p15: rule of law, democracy and participation of the people;
p15: (b) human dignity, equity, social justice, inclusiveness, equality,
p15: human rights, non-discrimination and protection of the
p15: marginalised;
p15: (c) good governance, integrity, transparency and accountability;
p15: and
p15: (d) sustainable development.
p15: Culture.
p15: 11. (1) This Constitution recognises culture as the foundation of
p15: the nation and as the cumulative civilization of the Kenyan people and
p15: nation.
p15: (2) The State shall—
p15: (a) promote all forms of national and cultural expression through
p15: literature, the arts, traditional celebrations, science,
p15: communication, information, mass media, publications,
p15: libraries and other cultural heritage;
p15: (b) recognise the role of science and indigenous technologies in
p15: the development of the nation; and
p15: (c) promote the intellectual property rights of the people of Kenya.
p15: (3) Parliament shall enact legislation to—
p15: (a) ensure that communities receive compensation or royalties for
p15: the use of their cultures and cultural heritage; and
p15: (b) recognise and protect the ownership of indigenous seeds and
p15: plant varieties, their genetic and diverse characteristics and
p15: their use by the communities of Kenya.
p15: CHAPTER THREE—CITIZENSHIP
p15: Entitlements of citizens.
p15: 12. (1) Every citizen is entitled to—
p15: (a) the rights, privileges and benefits of citizenship, subject to the
p15: limits provided or permitted by this Constitution; and
p15: (b) a Kenyan passport and any document of registration or
p15: identification issued by the State to citizens.
p16: (2) A passport or other document referred to in clause (1) (b) may
p16: be denied, suspended or confiscated only in accordance with an Act of
p16: Parliament that satisfies the criteria referred to in Article 24.
p16: Retention and acquisition of citizenship.
p16: 13. (1) Every person who was a citizen immediately before the
p16: effective date retains the same citizenship status as of that date.
p16: (2) Citizenship may be acquired by birth or registration.
p16: (3) Citizenship is not lost through marriage or the dissolution of
p16: marriage.
p16: Citizenship by birth.
p16: 14. (1) A person is a citizen by birth if on the day of the person’s
p16: birth, whether or not the person is born in Kenya, either the mother or
p16: father of the person is a citizen.
p16: (2) Clause (1) applies equally to a person born before the
p16: effective date, whether or not the person was born in Kenya, if either
p16: the mother or father of the person is or was a citizen.
p16: (3) Parliament may enact legislation limiting the effect of clauses
p16: (1) and (2) on the descendents of Kenyan citizens who are born
p16: outside Kenya.
p16: (4) A child found in Kenya who is, or appears to be, less than
p16: eight years of age, and whose nationality and parents are not known,
p16: is presumed to be a citizen by birth.
p16: (5) A person who is a Kenyan citizen by birth and who, on the
p16: effective date, has ceased to be a Kenyan citizen because the person
p16: acquired citizenship of another country, is entitled on application to
p16: regain Kenyan citizenship.
p16: Citizenship by registration.
p16: 15. (1) A person who has been married to a citizen for a period of
p16: at least seven years is entitled on application to be registered as a
p16: citizen.
p16: (2) A person who has been lawfully resident in Kenya for a
p16: continuous period of at least seven years, and who satisfies the
p16: conditions prescribed by an Act of Parliament, may apply to be
p16: registered as a citizen.
p16: (3) A child who is not a citizen, but is adopted by a citizen, is
p16: entitled on application to be registered as a citizen.
p17: (4) Parliament shall enact legislation establishing conditions on
p17: which citizenship may be granted to individuals who are citizens of
p17: other countries.
p17: (5) This Article applies to a person as from the effective date, but
p17: any requirements that must be satisfied before the person is entitled to
p17: be registered as a citizen shall be regarded as having been satisfied
p17: irrespective of whether the person satisfied them before or after the
p17: effective date, or partially before, and partially after, the effective date.
p17: Dual citizenship.
p17: 16. A citizen by birth does not lose citizenship by acquiring the
p17: citizenship of another country.
p17: Revocation of citizenship.
p17: 17. (1) If a person acquired citizenship by registration, the
p17: citizenship may be revoked if the person—
p17: (a) acquired the citizenship by fraud, false representation or
p17: concealment of any material fact;
p17: (b) has, during any war in which Kenya was engaged, unlawfully
p17: traded or communicated with an enemy or been engaged in
p17: or associated with any business that was knowingly carried
p17: on in such a manner as to assist an enemy in that war;
p17: (c) has, within five years after registration, been convicted of an
p17: offence and sentenced to imprisonment for a term of three
p17: years or longer; or
p17: (d) has, at any time after registration, been convicted of treason,
p17: or of an offence for which—
p17: (i) a penalty of at least seven years imprisonment may be
p17: imposed; or
p17: (ii) a more severe penalty may be imposed.
p17: (2) The citizenship of a person who was presumed to be a citizen
p17: by birth, as contemplated in Article 14 (4), may be revoked if—
p17: (a) the citizenship was acquired by fraud, false representation or
p17: concealment of any material fact by any person;
p17: (b) the nationality or parentage of the person becomes known,
p17: and reveals that the person was a citizen of another country;
p17: or
p18: (c) the age of the person becomes known, and reveals that the
p18: person was older than eight years when found in Kenya.
p18: Legislation on citizenship.
p18: 18. Parliament shall enact legislation—
p18: (a) prescribing procedures by which a person may become a
p18: citizen;
p18: (b) governing entry into and residence in Kenya;
p18: (c) providing for the status of permanent residents;
p18: (d) providing for voluntary renunciation of citizenship;
p18: (e) prescribing procedures for revocation of citizenship;
p18: (f) prescribing the duties and rights of citizens; and
p18: (g) generally giving effect to the provisions of this Chapter.
p18: CHAPTER FOUR—THE BILL OF RIGHTS
p18: PART 1—GENERAL PROVISIONS RELATING TO THE BILL OF RIGHTS
p18: Rights and fundamental freedoms.
p18: 19. (1) The Bill of Rights is an integral part of Kenya’s democratic
p18: state and is the framework for social, economic and cultural policies.
p18: (2) The purpose of recognising and protecting human rights and
p18: fundamental freedoms is to preserve the dignity of individuals and
p18: communities and to promote social justice and the realisation of the
p18: potential of all human beings.
p18: (3) The rights and fundamental freedoms in the Bill of Rights—
p18: (a) belong to each individual and are not granted by the State;
p18: (b) do not exclude other rights and fundamental freedoms not in
p18: the Bill of Rights, but recognised or conferred by law, except
p18: to the extent that they are inconsistent with this Chapter; and
p18: (c) are subject only to the limitations contemplated in this
p18: Constitution.
p18: Application of Bill of Rights.
p18: 20. (1) The Bill of Rights applies to all law and binds all State
p18: organs and all persons.
p19: (2) Every person shall enjoy the rights and fundamental freedoms
p19: in the Bill of Rights to the greatest extent consistent with the nature of
p19: the right or fundamental freedom.
p19: (3) In applying a provision of the Bill of Rights, a court shall—
p19: (a) develop the law to the extent that it does not give effect to a
p19: right or fundamental freedom; and
p19: (b) adopt the interpretation that most favours the enforcement of
p19: a right or fundamental freedom.
p19: (4) In interpreting the Bill of Rights, a court, tribunal or other
p19: authority shall promote—
p19: (a) the values that underlie an open and democratic society
p19: based on human dignity, equality, equity and freedom; and
p19: (b) the spirit, purport and objects of the Bill of Rights.
p19: (5) In applying any right under Article 43, if the State claims that it
p19: does not have the resources to implement the right, a court, tribunal or
p19: other authority shall be guided by the following principles—
p19: (a) it is the responsibility of the State to show that the resources
p19: are not available;
p19: (b) in allocating resources, the State shall give priority to ensuring
p19: the widest possible enjoyment of the right or fundamental
p19: freedom having regard to prevailing circumstances, including
p19: the vulnerability of particular groups or individuals; and
p19: (c) the court, tribunal or other authority may not interfere with a
p19: decision by a State organ concerning the allocation of
p19: available resources, solely on the basis that it would have
p19: reached a different conclusion.
p19: Implementation of rights and fundamental freedoms.
p19: 21. (1) It is a fundamental duty of the State and every State organ
p19: to observe, respect, protect, promote and fulfil the rights and
p19: fundamental freedoms in the Bill of Rights.
p19: (2) The State shall take legislative, policy and other measures,
p19: including the setting of standards, to achieve the progressive
p19: realisation of the rights guaranteed under Article 43.
p19: (3) All State organs and all public officers have the duty to
p19: address the needs of vulnerable groups within society, including
p19: women, older members of society, persons with disabilities, children,
p20: youth, members of minority or marginalised communities, and
p20: members of particular ethnic, religious or cultural communities.
p20: (4) The State shall enact and implement legislation to fulfil its
p20: international obligations in respect of human rights and fundamental
p20: freedoms.
p20: Enforcement of Bill of Rights.
p20: 22. (1) Every person has the right to institute court proceedings
p20: claiming that a right or fundamental freedom in the Bill of Rights has
p20: been denied, violated or infringed, or is threatened.
p20: (2) In addition to a person acting in their own interest, court
p20: proceedings under clause (1) may be instituted by—
p20: (a) a person acting on behalf of another person who cannot act
p20: in their own name;
p20: (b) a person acting as a member of, or in the interest of, a group
p20: or class of persons;
p20: (c) a person acting in the public interest; or
p20: (d) an association acting in the interest of one or more of its
p20: members.
p20: (3) The Chief Justice shall make rules providing for the court
p20: proceedings referred to in this Article, which shall satisfy the criteria
p20: that—
p20: (a) the rights of standing provided for in clause (2) are fully
p20: facilitated;
p20: (b) formalities relating to the proceedings, including
p20: commencement of the proceedings, are kept to the
p20: minimum, and in particular that the court shall, if necessary,
p20: entertain proceedings on the basis of informal documentation;
p20: (c) no fee may be charged for commencing the proceedings;
p20: (d) the court, while observing the rules of natural justice, shall not
p20: be unreasonably restricted by procedural technicalities; and
p20: (e) an organisation or individual with particular expertise may,
p20: with the leave of the court, appear as a friend of the court.
p20: (4) The absence of rules contemplated in clause (3) does not limit
p20: the right of any person to commence court proceedings under this
p20: Article, and to have the matter heard and determined by a court.
p21: Authority of courts to uphold and enforce the Bill of Rights.
p21: 23. (1) The High Court has jurisdiction, in accordance with Article
p21: 165, to hear and determine applications for redress of a denial,
p21: violation or infringement of, or threat to, a right or fundamental freedom
p21: in the Bill of Rights.
p21: (2) Parliament shall enact legislation to give original jurisdiction in
p21: appropriate cases to subordinate courts to hear and determine
p21: applications for redress of a denial, violation or infringement of, or
p21: threat to, a right or fundamental freedom in the Bill of Rights.
p21: (3) In any proceedings brought under Article 22, a court may
p21: grant appropriate relief, including—
p21: (a) a declaration of rights;
p21: (b) an injunction;
p21: (c) a conservatory order;
p21: (d) a declaration of invalidity of any law that denies, violates,
p21: infringes, or threatens a right or fundamental freedom in the
p21: Bill of Rights and is not justified under Article 24;
p21: (e) an order for compensation; and
p21: (f) an order of judicial review.
p21: Limitation of rights and fundamental freedoms.
p21: 24. (1) A right or fundamental freedom in the Bill of Rights shall
p21: not be limited except by law, and then only to the extent that the
p21: limitation is reasonable and justifiable in an open and democratic
p21: society based on human dignity, equality and freedom, taking into
p21: account all relevant factors, including—
p21: (a) the nature of the right or fundamental freedom;
p21: (b) the importance of the purpose of the limitation;
p21: (c) the nature and extent of the limitation;
p21: (d) the need to ensure that the enjoyment of rights and
p21: fundamental freedoms by any individual does not prejudice
p21: the rights and fundamental freedoms of others; and
p21: (e) the relation between the limitation and its purpose and
p21: whether there are less restrictive means to achieve the
p21: purpose.
p22: (2) Despite clause (1), a provision in legislation limiting a right or
p22: fundamental freedom—
p22: (a) in the case of a provision enacted or amended on or after the
p22: effective date, is not valid unless the legislation specifically
p22: expresses the intention to limit that right or fundamental
p22: freedom, and the nature and extent of the limitation;
p22: (b) shall not be construed as limiting the right or fundamental
p22: freedom unless the provision is clear and specific about the
p22: right or freedom to be limited and the nature and extent of the
p22: limitation; and
p22: (c) shall not limit the right or fundamental freedom so far as to
p22: derogate from its core or essential content.
p22: (3) The State or a person seeking to justify a particular limitation
p22: shall demonstrate to the court, tribunal or other authority that the
p22: requirements of this Article have been satisfied.
p22: (4) The provisions of this Chapter on equality shall be qualified to
p22: the extent strictly necessary for the application of Muslim law before
p22: the Kadhis’ courts, to persons who profess the Muslim religion, in
p22: matters relating to personal status, marriage, divorce and inheritance.
p22: (5) Despite clause (1) and (2), a provision in legislation may limit
p22: the application of the rights or fundamental freedoms in the following
p22: provisions to persons serving in the Kenya Defence Forces or the
p22: National Police Service—
p22: (a) Article 31—Privacy;
p22: (b) Article 36—Freedom of association;
p22: (c) Article 37—Assembly, demonstration, picketing and petition;
p22: (d) Article 41—Labour relations;
p22: (e) Article 43—Economic and social rights; and
p22: (f) Article 49—Rights of arrested persons.
p22: Fundamental Rights and freedoms that may not be limited.
p22: 25. Despite any other provision in this Constitution, the following
p22: rights and fundamental freedoms shall not be limited—
p22: (a) freedom from torture and cruel, inhuman or degrading
p22: treatment or punishment;
p23: (b) freedom from slavery or servitude;
p23: (c) the right to a fair trial; and
p23: (d) the right to an order of habeas corpus.
p23: PART 2—RIGHTS AND FUNDAMENTAL FREEDOMS
p23: Right to life.
p23: 26. (1) Every person has the right to life.
p23: (2) The life of a person begins at conception.
p23: (3) A person shall not be deprived of life intentionally, except to
p23: the extent authorised by this Constitution or other written law.
p23: (4) Abortion is not permitted unless, in the opinion of a trained
p23: health professional, there is need for emergency treatment, or the life
p23: or health of the mother is in danger, or if permitted by any other written
p23: law.
p23: Equality and freedom from discrimination.
p23: 27. (1) Every person is equal before the law and has the right to
p23: equal protection and equal benefit of the law.
p23: (2) Equality includes the full and equal enjoyment of all rights and
p23: fundamental freedoms.
p23: (3) Women and men have the right to equal treatment, including
p23: the right to equal opportunities in political, economic, cultural and
p23: social spheres.
p23: (4) The State shall not discriminate directly or indirectly against
p23: any person on any ground, including race, sex, pregnancy, marital
p23: status, health status, ethnic or social origin, colour, age, disability,
p23: religion, conscience, belief, culture, dress, language or birth.
p23: (5) A person shall not discriminate directly or indirectly against
p23: another person on any of the grounds specified or contemplated in
p23: clause (4).
p23: (6) To give full effect to the realisation of the rights guaranteed
p23: under this Article, the State shall take legislative and other measures,
p23: including affirmative action programmes and policies designed to
p23: redress any disadvantage suffered by individuals or groups because of
p23: past discrimination.
p24: (7) Any measure taken under clause (6) shall adequately provide
p24: for any benefits to be on the basis of genuine need.
p24: (8) In addition to the measures contemplated in clause (6), the
p24: State shall take legislative and other measures to implement the
p24: principle that not more than two-thirds of the members of elective or
p24: appointive bodies shall be of the same gender.
p24: Human dignity.
p24: 28. Every person has inherent dignity and the right to have that
p24: dignity respected and protected.
p24: Freedom and security of the person.
p24: 29. Every person has the right to freedom and security of the
p24: person, which includes the right not to be—
p24: (a) deprived of freedom arbitrarily or without just cause;
p24: (b) detained without trial, except during a state of emergency, in
p24: which case the detention is subject to Article 58;
p24: (c) subjected to any form of violence from either public or private
p24: sources;
p24: (d) subjected to torture in any manner, whether physical or
p24: psychological;
p24: (e) subjected to corporal punishment; or
p24: (f) treated or punished in a cruel, inhuman or degrading manner.
p24: Slavery, servitude and forced labour.
p24: 30. (1) A person shall not be held in slavery or servitude.
p24: (2) A person shall not be required to perform forced labour.
p24: Privacy.
p24: 31. Every person has the right to privacy, which includes the right
p24: not to have—
p24: (a) their person, home or property searched;
p24: (b) their possessions seized;
p24: (c) information relating to their family or private affairs
p24: unnecessarily required or revealed; or
p24: (d) the privacy of their communications infringed.
p25: Freedom of conscience, religion, belief and opinion.
p25: 32. (1) Every person has the right to freedom of conscience,
p25: religion, thought, belief and opinion.
p25: (2) Every person has the right, either individually or in community
p25: with others, in public or in private, to manifest any religion or belief
p25: through worship, practice, teaching or observance, including
p25: observance of a day of worship.
p25: (3) A person may not be denied access to any institution,
p25: employment or facility, or the enjoyment of any right, because of the
p25: person’s belief or religion.
p25: (4) A person shall not be compelled to act, or engage in any act,
p25: that is contrary to the person’s belief or religion.
p25: Freedom of expression.
p25: 33. (1) Every person has the right to freedom of expression,
p25: which includes—
p25: (a) freedom to seek, receive or impart information or ideas;
p25: (b) freedom of artistic creativity; and
p25: (c) academic freedom and freedom of scientific research.
p25: (2) The right to freedom of expression does not extend to—
p25: (a) propaganda for war;
p25: (b) incitement to violence;
p25: (c) hate speech; or
p25: (d) advocacy of hatred that—
p25: (i) constitutes ethnic incitement, vilification of others or
p25: incitement to cause harm; or
p25: (ii) is based on any ground of discrimination specified or
p25: contemplated in Article 27 (4).
p25: (3) In the exercise of the right to freedom of expression, every
p25: person shall respect the rights and reputation of others.
p25: Freedom of the media.
p25: 34. (1) Freedom and independence of electronic, print and all
p25: other types of media is guaranteed, but does not extend to any
p25: expression specified in Article 33 (2).
p26: (2) The State shall not—
p26: (a) exercise control over or interfere with any person engaged in
p26: broadcasting, the production or circulation of any publication
p26: or the dissemination of information by any medium; or
p26: (b) penalise any person for any opinion or view or the content of
p26: any broadcast, publication or dissemination.
p26: (3) Broadcasting and other electronic media have freedom of
p26: establishment, subject only to licensing procedures that—
p26: (a) are necessary to regulate the airwaves and other forms of
p26: signal distribution; and
p26: (b) are independent of control by government, political interests
p26: or commercial interests.
p26: (4) All State-owned media shall—
p26: (a) be free to determine independently the editorial content of
p26: their broadcasts or other communications;
p26: (b) be impartial; and
p26: (c) afford fair opportunity for the presentation of divergent views
p26: and dissenting opinions.
p26: (5) Parliament shall enact legislation that provides for the
p26: establishment of a body, which shall—
p26: (a) be independent of control by government, political interests or
p26: commercial interests;
p26: (b) reflect the interests of all sections of the society; and
p26: (c) set media standards and regulate and monitor compliance
p26: with those standards.
p26: Access to information.
p26: 35. (1) Every citizen has the right of access to—
p26: (a) information held by the State; and
p26: (b) information held by another person and required for the
p26: exercise or protection of any right or fundamental freedom.
p26: (2) Every person has the right to the correction or deletion of
p26: untrue or misleading information that affects the person.
p27: (3) The State shall publish and publicise any important
p27: information affecting the nation.
p27: Freedom of association.
p27: 36. (1) Every person has the right to freedom of association,
p27: which includes the right to form, join or participate in the activities of an
p27: association of any kind.
p27: (2) A person shall not be compelled to join an association of any
p27: kind.
p27: (3) Any legislation that requires registration of an association of
p27: any kind shall provide that—
p27: (a) registration may not be withheld or withdrawn unreasonably;
p27: and
p27: (b) there shall be a right to have a fair hearing before a
p27: registration is cancelled.
p27: Assembly, demonstration, picketing and petition.
p27: 37. Every person has the right, peaceably and unarmed, to
p27: assemble, to demonstrate, to picket, and to present petitions to public
p27: authorities.
p27: Political rights.
p27: 38. (1) Every citizen is free to make political choices, which
p27: includes the right—
p27: (a) to form, or participate in forming, a political party;
p27: (b) to participate in the activities of, or recruit members for, a
p27: political party; or
p27: (c) to campaign for a political party or cause.
p27: (2) Every citizen has the right to free, fair and regular elections
p27: based on universal suffrage and the free expression of the will of the
p27: electors for—
p27: (a) any elective public body or office established under this
p27: Constitution; or
p27: (b) any office of any political party of which the citizen is a
p27: member.
p27: (3) Every adult citizen has the right, without unreasonable
p27: restrictions—
p28: (a) to be registered as a voter;
p28: (b) to vote by secret ballot in any election or referendum; and
p28: (c) to be a candidate for public office, or office within a political
p28: party of which the citizen is a member and, if elected, to hold
p28: office.
p28: Freedom of movement and residence.
p28: 39. (1) Every person has the right to freedom of movement.
p28: (2) Every person has the right to leave Kenya.
p28: (3) Every citizen has the right to enter, remain in and reside
p28: anywhere in Kenya.
p28: Protection of right to property.
p28: 40. (1) Subject to Article 65, every person has the right, either
p28: individually or in association with others, to acquire and own property
p28: —
p28: (a) of any description; and
p28: (b) in any part of Kenya.
p28: (2) Parliament shall not enact a law that permits the State or any
p28: person—
p28: (a) to arbitrarily deprive a person of property of any description or
p28: of any interest in, or right over, any property of any
p28: description; or
p28: (b) to limit, or in any way restrict the enjoyment of any right under
p28: this Article on the basis of any of the grounds specified or
p28: contemplated in Article 27 (4).
p28: (3) The State shall not deprive a person of property of any
p28: description, or of any interest in, or right over, property of any
p28: description, unless the deprivation—
p28: (a) results from an acquisition of land or an interest in land or a
p28: conversion of an interest in land, or title to land, in
p28: accordance with Chapter Five; or
p28: (b) is for a public purpose or in the public interest and is carried
p28: out in accordance with this Constitution and any Act of
p28: Parliament that—
p29: (i) requires prompt payment in full, of just compensation to
p29: the person; and
p29: (ii) allows any person who has an interest in, or right over,
p29: that property a right of access to a court of law.
p29: (4) Provision may be made for compensation to be paid to
p29: occupants in good faith of land acquired under clause (3) who may not
p29: hold title to the land.
p29: (5) The State shall support, promote and protect the intellectual
p29: property rights of the people of Kenya.
p29: (6) The rights under this Article do not extend to any property that
p29: has been found to have been unlawfully acquired.
p29: Labour relations.
p29: 41. (1) Every person has the right to fair labour practices.
p29: (2) Every worker has the right—
p29: (a) to fair remuneration;
p29: (b) to reasonable working conditions;
p29: (c) to form, join or participate in the activities and programmes of
p29: a trade union; and
p29: (d) to go on strike.
p29: (3) Every employer has the right—
p29: (e) to form and join an employers organisation; and
p29: (f) to participate in the activities and programmes of an employers
p29: organisation.
p29: (4) Every trade union and every employers’ organisation has the
p29: right—
p29: (a) to determine its own administration, programmes and
p29: activities;
p29: (b) to organise; and
p29: (c) to form and join a federation.
p29: (5) Every trade union, employers’ organisation and employer has
p29: the right to engage in collective bargaining.
p30: Environment.
p30: 42. Every person has the right to a clean and healthy
p30: environment, which includes the right—
p30: (a) to have the environment protected for the benefit of present
p30: and future generations through legislative and other
p30: measures, particularly those contemplated in Article 69; and
p30: (b) to have obligations relating to the environment fulfilled under
p30: Article 70.
p30: Economic and social rights.
p30: 43. (1) Every person has the right—
p30: (a) to the highest attainable standard of health, which includes
p30: the right to health care services, including reproductive health
p30: care;
p30: (b) to accessible and adequate housing, and to reasonable
p30: standards of sanitation;
p30: (c) to be free from hunger, and to have adequate food of
p30: acceptable quality;
p30: (d) to clean and safe water in adequate quantities;
p30: (e) to social security; and
p30: (f) to education.
p30: (2) A person shall not be denied emergency medical treatment.
p30: (3) The State shall provide appropriate social security to persons
p30: who are unable to support themselves and their dependants.
p30: Language and culture.
p30: 44. (1) Every person has the right to use the language, and to
p30: participate in the cultural life, of the person’s choice.
p30: (2) A person belonging to a cultural or linguistic community has
p30: the right, with other members of that community—
p30: (a) to enjoy the person’s culture and use the person’s language;
p30: or
p30: (b) to form, join and maintain cultural and linguistic associations
p30: and other organs of civil society.
p30: (3) A person shall not compel another person to perform, observe
p30: or undergo any cultural practice or rite.
p31: Family.
p31: 45. (1) The family is the natural and fundamental unit of society
p31: and the necessary basis of social order, and shall enjoy the recognition
p31: and protection of the State.
p31: (2) Every adult has the right to marry a person of the opposite
p31: sex, based on the free consent of the parties.
p31: (3) Parties to a marriage are entitled to equal rights at the time of
p31: the marriage, during the marriage and at the dissolution of the
p31: marriage.
p31: (4) Parliament shall enact legislation that recognises—
p31: (a) marriages concluded under any tradition, or system of
p31: religious, personal or family law; and
p31: (b) any system of personal and family law under any tradition, or
p31: adhered to by persons professing a particular religion,
p31: to the extent that any such marriages or systems of law are consistent
p31: with this Constitution.
p31: Consumer rights.
p31: 46. (1) Consumers have the right—
p31: (a) to goods and services of reasonable quality;
p31: (b) to the information necessary for them to gain full benefit from
p31: goods and services;
p31: (c) to the protection of their health, safety, and economic
p31: interests; and
p31: (d) to compensation for loss or injury arising from defects in
p31: goods or services.
p31: (2) Parliament shall enact legislation to provide for consumer
p31: protection and for fair, honest and decent advertising.
p31: (3) This Article applies to goods and services offered by public
p31: entities or private persons.
p31: Fair administrative action.
p31: 47. (1) Every person has the right to administrative action that is
p31: expeditious, efficient, lawful, reasonable and procedurally fair.
p31: (2) If a right or fundamental freedom of a person has been or is
p32: likely to be adversely affected by administrative action, the person has
p32: the right to be given written reasons for the action.
p32: (3) Parliament shall enact legislation to give effect to the rights in
p32: clause (1) and that legislation shall—
p32: (a) provide for the review of administrative action by a court or, if
p32: appropriate, an independent and impartial tribunal; and
p32: (b) promote efficient administration.
p32: Access to justice.
p32: 48. The State shall ensure access to justice for all persons and, if
p32: any fee is required, it shall be reasonable and shall not impede access
p32: to justice.
p32: Rights of arrested persons.
p32: 49. (1) An arrested person has the right—
p32: (a) to be informed promptly, in language that the person
p32: understands, of—
p32: (i) the reason for the arrest;
p32: (ii) the right to remain silent; and
p32: (iii) the consequences of not remaining silent;
p32: (b) to remain silent;
p32: (c) to communicate with an advocate, and other persons whose
p32: assistance is necessary;
p32: (d) not to be compelled to make any confession or admission that
p32: could be used in evidence against the person;
p32: (e) to be held separately from persons who are serving a
p32: sentence;
p32: (f) to be brought before a court as soon as reasonably possible,
p32: but not later than—
p32: (i) twenty-four hours after being arrested; or
p32: (ii) if the twenty-four hours ends outside ordinary court hours,
p32: or on a day that is not an ordinary court day, the end of
p32: the next court day;
p32: (g) at the first court appearance, to be charged or informed of the
p32: reason for the detention continuing, or to be released; and
p33: (h) to be released on bond or bail, on reasonable conditions,
p33: pending a charge or trial, unless there are compelling
p33: reasons not to be released.
p33: (2) A person shall not be remanded in custody for an offence if
p33: the offence is punishable by a fine only or by imprisonment for not
p33: more than six months.
p33: Fair hearing.
p33: 50. (1) Every person has the right to have any dispute that can be
p33: resolved by the application of law decided in a fair and public hearing
p33: before a court or, if appropriate, another independent and impartial
p33: tribunal or body.
p33: (2) Every accused person has the right to a fair trial, which
p33: includes the right—
p33: (a) to be presumed innocent until the contrary is proved;
p33: (b) to be informed of the charge, with sufficient detail to answer it;
p33: (c) to have adequate time and facilities to prepare a defence;
p33: (d) to a public trial before a court established under this
p33: Constitution;
p33: (e) to have the trial begin and conclude without unreasonable
p33: delay;
p33: (f) to be present when being tried, unless the conduct of the
p33: accused person makes it impossible for the trial to proceed;
p33: (g) to choose, and be represented by, an advocate, and to be
p33: informed of this right promptly;
p33: (h) to have an advocate assigned to the accused person by the
p33: State and at State expense, if substantial injustice would
p33: otherwise result, and to be informed of this right promptly;
p33: (i) to remain silent, and not to testify during the proceedings;
p33: (j) to be informed in advance of the evidence the prosecution
p33: intends to rely on, and to have reasonable access to that
p33: evidence;
p33: (k) to adduce and challenge evidence;
p33: (l) to refuse to give self-incriminating evidence;
p34: (m) to have the assistance of an interpreter without payment if
p34: the accused person cannot understand the language used at
p34: the trial;
p34: (n) not to be convicted for an act or omission that at the time it
p34: was committed or omitted was not—
p34: (i) an offence in Kenya; or
p34: (ii) a crime under international law;
p34: (o) not to be tried for an offence in respect of an act or omission
p34: for which the accused person has previously been either
p34: acquitted or convicted;
p34: (p) to the benefit of the least severe of the prescribed
p34: punishments for an offence, if the prescribed punishment for
p34: the offence has been changed between the time that the
p34: offence was committed and the time of sentencing; and
p34: (q) if convicted, to appeal to, or apply for review by, a higher court
p34: as prescribed by law.
p34: (3) If this Article requires information to be given to a person, the
p34: information shall be given in language that the person understands.
p34: (4) Evidence obtained in a manner that violates any right or
p34: fundamental freedom in the Bill of Rights shall be excluded if the
p34: admission of that evidence would render the trial unfair, or would
p34: otherwise be detrimental to the administration of justice.
p34: (5) An accused person—
p34: (a) charged with an offence, other than an offence that the court
p34: may try by summary procedures, is entitled during the trial to
p34: a copy of the record of the proceedings of the trial on request;
p34: and
p34: (b) has the right to a copy of the record of the proceedings within
p34: a reasonable period after they are concluded, in return for a
p34: reasonable fee as prescribed by law.
p34: (6) A person who is convicted of a criminal offence may petition
p34: the High Court for a new trial if—
p34: (a) the person’s appeal, if any, has been dismissed by the
p34: highest court to which the person is entitled to appeal, or the
p34: person did not appeal within the time allowed for appeal; and
p34: (b) new and compelling evidence has become available.
p35: (7) In the interest of justice, a court may allow an intermediary to
p35: assist a complainant or an accused person to communicate with the
p35: court.
p35: (8) This Article does not prevent the exclusion of the press or
p35: other members of the public from any proceedings if the exclusion is
p35: necessary, in a free and democratic society, to protect witnesses or
p35: vulnerable persons, morality, public order or national security.
p35: (9) Parliament shall enact legislation providing for the protection,
p35: rights and welfare of victims of offences.
p35: Rights of persons detained, held in custody or imprisoned.
p35: 51. (1) A person who is detained, held in custody or imprisoned
p35: under the law, retains all the rights and fundamental freedoms in the
p35: Bill of Rights, except to the extent that any particular right or a
p35: fundamental freedom is clearly incompatible with the fact that the
p35: person is detained, held in custody or imprisoned.
p35: (2) A person who is detained or held in custody is entitled to
p35: petition for an order of habeas corpus.
p35: (3) Parliament shall enact legislation that—
p35: (a) provides for the humane treatment of persons detained, held
p35: in custody or imprisoned; and
p35: (b) takes into account the relevant international human rights
p35: instruments.
p35: PART 3— SPECIFIC APPLICATION OF RIGHTS
p35: Interpretation of this Part.
p35: 52. (1) This Part elaborates certain rights to ensure greater
p35: certainty as to the application of those rights and fundamental
p35: freedoms to certain groups of persons.
p35: (2) This Part shall not be construed as limiting or qualifying any
p35: right.
p35: Children.
p35: 53. (1) Every child has the right—
p35: (a) to a name and nationality from birth;
p35: (b) to free and compulsory basic education;
p35: (c) to basic nutrition, shelter and health care;
p36: (d) to be protected from abuse, neglect, harmful cultural
p36: practices, all forms of violence, inhuman treatment and
p36: punishment, and hazardous or exploitative labour;
p36: (e) to parental care and protection, which includes equal
p36: responsibility of the mother and father to provide for the child,
p36: whether they are married to each other or not; and
p36: (f) not to be detained, except as a measure of last resort, and
p36: when detained, to be held—
p36: (i) for the shortest appropriate period of time; and
p36: (ii) separate from adults and in conditions that take account
p36: of the child’s sex and age.
p36: (2) A child’s best interests are of paramount importance in every
p36: matter concerning the child.
p36: Persons with disabilities.
p36: 54. (1) A person with any disability is entitled—
p36: (a) to be treated with dignity and respect and to be addressed
p36: and referred to in a manner that is not demeaning;
p36: (b) to access educational institutions and facilities for persons
p36: with disabilities that are integrated into society to the extent
p36: compatible with the interests of the person;
p36: (c) to reasonable access to all places, public transport and
p36: information;
p36: (d) to use Sign language, Braille or other appropriate means of
p36: communication; and
p36: (e) to access materials and devices to overcome constraints
p36: arising from the person’s disability.
p36: (2) The State shall ensure the progressive implementation of the
p36: principle that at least five percent of the members of the public in
p36: elective and appointive bodies are persons with disabilities.
p36: Youth.
p36: 55. The State shall take measures, including affirmative action
p36: programmes, to ensure that the youth—
p36: (a) access relevant education and training;
p37: (b) have opportunities to associate, be represented and
p37: participate in political, social, economic and other spheres of
p37: life;
p37: (c) access employment; and
p37: (d) are protected from harmful cultural practices and exploitation.
p37: Minorities and marginalised groups.
p37: 56. The State shall put in place affirmative action programmes
p37: designed to ensure that minorities and marginalised groups—
p37: (a) participate and are represented in governance and other
p37: spheres of life;
p37: (b) are provided special opportunities in educational and
p37: economic fields;
p37: (c) are provided special opportunities for access to employment;
p37: (d) develop their cultural values, languages and practices; and
p37: (e) have reasonable access to water, health services and
p37: infrastructure.
p37: Older members of society.
p37: 57. The State shall take measures to ensure the rights of older
p37: persons—
p37: (a) to fully participate in the affairs of society;
p37: (b) to pursue their personal development;
p37: (c) to live in dignity and respect and be free from abuse; and
p37: (d) to receive reasonable care and assistance from their family
p37: and the State.
p37: PART 4—STATE OF EMERGENCY
p37: State of emergency.
p37: 58. (1) A state of emergency may be declared only under Article
p37: 132 (4) (d) and only when—
p37: (a) the State is threatened by war, invasion, general insurrection,
p37: disorder, natural disaster or other public emergency; and
p37: (b) the declaration is necessary to meet the circumstances for
p37: which the emergency is declared.
p38: (2) A declaration of a state of emergency, and any legislation
p38: enacted or other action taken in consequence of the declaration, shall
p38: be effective only—
p38: (a) prospectively; and
p38: (b) for not longer than fourteen days from the date of the
p38: declaration, unless the National Assembly resolves to extend
p38: the declaration.
p38: (3) The National Assembly may extend a declaration of a state of
p38: emergency—
p38: (a) by resolution adopted—
p38: (i) following a public debate in the National Assembly; and
p38: (ii) by the majorities specified in clause (4); and
p38: (b) for not longer than two months at a time.
p38: (4) The first extension of the declaration of a state of emergency
p38: requires a supporting vote of at least two-thirds of all the members of
p38: the National Assembly, and any subsequent extension requires a
p38: supporting vote of at least three-quarters of all the members of the
p38: National Assembly.
p38: (5) The Supreme Court may decide on the validity of—
p38: (a) a declaration of a state of emergency;
p38: (b) any extension of a declaration of a state of emergency; and
p38: (c) any legislation enacted, or other action taken, in consequence
p38: of a declaration of a state of emergency.
p38: (6) Any legislation enacted in consequence of a declaration of a
p38: state of emergency—
p38: (a) may limit a right or fundamental freedom in the Bill of Rights
p38: only to the extent that—
p38: (i) the limitation is strictly required by the emergency; and
p38: (ii) the legislation is consistent with the Republic’s obligations
p38: under international law applicable to a state of
p38: emergency; and
p38: (b) shall not take effect until it is published in the Gazette.
p38: (7) A declaration of a state of emergency, or legislation enacted
p39: or other action taken in consequence of any declaration, may not
p39: permit or authorise the indemnification of the State, or of any person,
p39: in respect of any unlawful act or omission.
p39: PART 5— KENYA NATIONAL HUMAN RIGHTS AND EQUALITY COMMISSION
p39: Kenya National Human Rights and Equality Commission.
p39: 59. (1) There is established the Kenya National Human Rights
p39: and Equality Commission.
p39: (2) The functions of the Commission are—
p39: (a) to promote respect for human rights and develop a culture of
p39: human rights in the Republic;
p39: (b) to promote gender equality and equity generally and to
p39: coordinate and facilitate gender mainstreaming in national
p39: development;
p39: (c) to promote the protection, and observance of human rights in
p39: public and private institutions;
p39: (d) to monitor, investigate and report on the observance of
p39: human rights in all spheres of life in the Republic, including
p39: observance by the national security organs;
p39: (e) to receive and investigate complaints about alleged abuses of
p39: human rights and take steps to secure appropriate redress
p39: where human rights have been violated;
p39: (f) on its own initiative or on the basis of complaints, to
p39: investigate or research a matter in respect of human rights,
p39: and make recommendations to improve the functioning of
p39: State organs;
p39: (g) to act as the principal organ of the State in ensuring
p39: compliance with obligations under treaties and conventions
p39: relating to human rights;
p39: (h) to investigate any conduct in state affairs, or any act or
p39: omission in public administration in any sphere of
p39: government, that is alleged or suspected to be prejudicial or
p39: improper or to result in any impropriety or prejudice;
p39: (i) to investigate complaints of abuse of power, unfair treatment,
p39: manifest injustice or unlawful, oppressive, unfair or
p39: unresponsive official conduct;
p40: (j) to report on complaints investigated under paragraphs (h) and
p40: (i) and take remedial action; and
p40: (k) to perform any other functions prescribed by legislation.
p40: (3) Every person has the right to complain to the Commission,
p40: alleging that a right or fundamental freedom in the Bill of Rights has
p40: been denied, violated or infringed, or is threatened.
p40: (4) Parliament shall enact legislation to give full effect to this Part,
p40: and any such legislation may restructure the Commission into two or
p40: more separate commissions.
p40: (5) If Parliament enacts legislation restructuring the Commission
p40: under clause (4)—
p40: (a) that legislation shall assign each function of the Commission
p40: specified in this Article to one or the other of the successor
p40: commissions;
p40: (b) each of the successor commissions shall have powers
p40: equivalent to the powers of the Commission under this
p40: Article; and
p40: (c) each successor commission shall be a commission within the
p40: meaning of Chapter Fifteen, and shall have the status and
p40: powers of a commission under that Chapter.
p40: CHAPTER FIVE—LAND AND ENVIRONMENT
p40: PART 1—LAND
p40: Principles of land policy.
p40: 60. (1) Land in Kenya shall be held, used and managed in a
p40: manner that is equitable, efficient, productive and sustainable, and in
p40: accordance with the following principles—
p40: (a) equitable access to land;
p40: (b) security of land rights;
p40: (c) sustainable and productive management of land resources;
p40: (d) transparent and cost effective administration of land;
p40: (e) sound conservation and protection of ecologically sensitive
p40: areas;
p41: (f) elimination of gender discrimination in law, customs and
p41: practices related to land and property in land; and
p41: (g) encouragement of communities to settle land disputes
p41: through recognised local community initiatives consistent with
p41: this Constitution.
p41: (2) These principles shall be implemented through a national land
p41: policy developed and reviewed regularly by the national government
p41: and through legislation.
p41: Classification of land.
p41: 61. (1) All land in Kenya belongs to the people of Kenya
p41: collectively as a nation, as communities and as individuals.
p41: (2) Land in Kenya is classified as public, community or private.
p41: Public land.
p41: 62. (1) Public land is—
p41: (a) land which at the effective date was unalienated government
p41: land as defined by an Act of Parliament in force at the
p41: effective date;
p41: (b) land lawfully held, used or occupied by any State organ,
p41: except any such land that is occupied by the State organ as
p41: lessee under a private lease;
p41: (c) land transferred to the State by way of sale, reversion or
p41: surrender;
p41: (d) land in respect of which no individual or community ownership
p41: can be established by any legal process;
p41: (e) land in respect of which no heir can be identified by any legal
p41: process;
p41: (f) all minerals and mineral oils as defined by law;
p41: (g) government forests other than forests to which Article 63 (2)
p41: (d) (i) applies, government game reserves, water catchment
p41: areas, national parks, government animal sanctuaries, and
p41: specially protected areas;
p41: (h) all roads and thoroughfares provided for by an Act of
p41: Parliament;
p41: (i) all rivers, lakes and other water bodies as defined by an Act
p41: of Parliament;
p42: (j) the territorial sea, the exclusive economic zone and the sea
p42: bed;
p42: (k) the continental shelf;
p42: (l) all land between the high and low water marks;
p42: (m) any land not classified as private or community land under
p42: this Constitution; and
p42: (n) any other land declared to be public land by an Act of
p42: Parliament—
p42: (i) in force at the effective date; or
p42: (ii) enacted after the effective date.
p42: (2) Public land shall vest in and be held by a county government
p42: in trust for the people resident in the county, and shall be administered
p42: on their behalf by the National Land Commission, if it is classified
p42: under—
p42: (a) clause (1) (a), (c), (d) or (e); and
p42: (b) clause (1) (b), other than land held, used or occupied by a
p42: national State organ.
p42: (3) Public land classified under clause (1) (f) to (m) shall vest in
p42: and be held by the national government in trust for the people of
p42: Kenya and shall be administered on their behalf by the National Land
p42: Commission.
p42: (4) Public land shall not be disposed of or otherwise used except
p42: in terms of an Act of Parliament specifying the nature and terms of that
p42: disposal or use.
p42: Community land.
p42: 63. (1) Community land shall vest in and be held by communities
p42: identified on the basis of ethnicity, culture or similar community of
p42: interest.
p42: (2) Community land consists of—
p42: (a) land lawfully registered in the name of group representatives
p42: under the provisions of any law;
p42: (b) land lawfully transferred to a specific community by any
p42: process of law;
p42: (c) any other land declared to be community land by an Act of
p43: Parliament; and
p43: (d) land that is—
p43: (i) lawfully held, managed or used by specific communities
p43: as community forests, grazing areas or shrines;
p43: (ii) ancestral lands and lands traditionally occupied by
p43: hunter-gatherer communities; or
p43: (iii) lawfully held as trust land by the county governments,
p43: but not including any public land held in trust by the county
p43: government under Article 62 (2).
p43: (3) Any unregistered community land shall be held in trust by
p43: county governments on behalf of the communities for which it is held.
p43: (4) Community land shall not be disposed of or otherwise used
p43: except in terms of legislation specifying the nature and extent of the
p43: rights of members of each community individually and collectively.
p43: (5) Parliament shall enact legislation to give effect to this Article.
p43: Private land.
p43: 64. Private land consists of —
p43: (a) registered land held by any person under any freehold tenure;
p43: (b) land held by any person under leasehold tenure; and
p43: (c) any other land declared private land under an Act of
p43: Parliament.
p43: Landholding by non-citizens.
p43: 65. (1) A person who is not a citizen may hold land on the basis
p43: of leasehold tenure only, and any such lease, however granted, shall
p43: not exceed ninety-nine years.
p43: (2) If a provision of any agreement, deed, conveyance or
p43: document of whatever nature purports to confer on a person who is not
p43: a citizen an interest in land greater than a ninety-nine year lease, the
p43: provision shall be regarded as conferring on the person a ninety-nine
p43: year leasehold interest, and no more.
p43: (3) For purposes of this Article—
p43: (a) a body corporate shall be regarded as a citizen only if the
p43: body corporate is wholly owned by one or more citizens; and
p44: (b) property held in trust shall be regarded as being held by a
p44: citizen only if all of the beneficial interest of the trust is held by
p44: persons who are citizens.
p44: (4) Parliament may enact legislation to make further provision for
p44: the operation of this Article.
p44: Regulation of land use and property.
p44: 66. (1) The State may regulate the use of any land, or any
p44: interest in or right over any land, in the interest of defence, public
p44: safety, public order, public morality, public health, or land use planning.
p44: (2) Parliament shall enact legislation ensuring that investments in
p44: property benefit local communities and their economies.
p44: National Land Commission.
p44: 67. (1) There is established the National Land Commission.
p44: (2) The functions of the National Land Commission are—
p44: (a) to manage public land on behalf of the national and county
p44: governments;
p44: (b) to recommend a national land policy to the national
p44: government;
p44: (c) to advise the national government on a comprehensive
p44: programme for the registration of title in land throughout
p44: Kenya;
p44: (d) to conduct research related to land and the use of natural
p44: resources, and make recommendations to appropriate
p44: authorities;
p44: (e) to initiate investigations, on its own initiative or on a complaint,
p44: into present or historical land injustices, and recommend
p44: appropriate redress;
p44: (f) to encourage the application of traditional dispute resolution
p44: mechanisms in land conflicts;
p44: (g) to assess tax on land and premiums on immovable property in
p44: any area designated by law; and
p44: (h) to monitor and have oversight responsibilities over land use
p44: planning throughout the country.
p44: (3) The National Land Commission may perform any other
p45: functions prescribed by national legislation.
p45: Legislation on land.
p45: 68. Parliament shall—
p45: (a) revise, consolidate and rationalise existing land laws;
p45: (b) revise sectoral land use laws in accordance with the principles
p45: set out in Article 60 (1); and
p45: (c) enact legislation—
p45: (i) to prescribe minimum and maximum land holding
p45: acreages in respect of private land;
p45: (ii) to regulate the manner in which any land may be
p45: converted from one category to another;
p45: (iii) to regulate the recognition and protection of matrimonial
p45: property and in particular the matrimonial home during
p45: and on the termination of marriage;
p45: (iv) to protect, conserve and provide access to all public land;
p45: (v) to enable the review of all grants or dispositions of public
p45: land to establish their propriety or legality;
p45: (vi) to protect the dependants of deceased persons holding
p45: interests in any land, including the interests of spouses
p45: in actual occupation of land; and
p45: (vii) to provide for any other matter necessary to give effect
p45: to the provisions of this Chapter.
p45: PART 2—ENVIRONMENT AND NATURAL RESOURCES
p45: Obligations in respect of the environment.
p45: 69. (1) The State shall—
p45: (a) ensure sustainable exploitation, utilisation, management and
p45: conservation of the environment and natural resources, and
p45: ensure the equitable sharing of the accruing benefits;
p45: (b) work to achieve and maintain a tree cover of at least ten per
p45: cent of the land area of Kenya;
p45: (c) protect and enhance intellectual property in, and indigenous
p45: knowledge of, biodiversity and the genetic resources of the
p45: communities;
p46: (d) encourage public participation in the management, protection
p46: and conservation of the environment;
p46: (e) protect genetic resources and biological diversity;
p46: (f) establish systems of environmental impact assessment,
p46: environmental audit and monitoring of the environment;
p46: (g) eliminate processes and activities that are likely to endanger
p46: the environment; and
p46: (h) utilise the environment and natural resources for the benefit of
p46: the people of Kenya.
p46: (2) Every person has a duty to cooperate with State organs and
p46: other persons to protect and conserve the environment and ensure
p46: ecologically sustainable development and use of natural resources.
p46: Enforcement of environmental rights.
p46: 70. (1) If a person alleges that a right to a clean and healthy
p46: environment recognised and protected under Article 42 has been, is
p46: being or is likely to be, denied, violated, infringed or threatened, the
p46: person may apply to a court for redress in addition to any other legal
p46: remedies that are available in respect to the same matter.
p46: (2) On application under clause (1), the court may make any
p46: order, or give any directions, it considers appropriate—
p46: (a) to prevent, stop or discontinue any act or omission that is
p46: harmful to the environment;
p46: (b) to compel any public officer to take measures to prevent or
p46: discontinue any act or omission that is harmful to the
p46: environment; or
p46: (c) to provide compensation for any victim of a violation of the
p46: right to a clean and healthy environment.
p46: (3) For the purposes of this Article, an applicant does not have to
p46: demonstrate that any person has incurred loss or suffered injury.
p46: Agreements relating to natural resource.
p46: 71. (1) A transaction is subject to ratification by Parliament if it—
p46: (a) involves the grant of a right or concession by or on behalf of
p46: any person, including the national government, to another
p46: person for the exploitation of any natural resource of Kenya;
p46: and
p47: (b) is entered into on or after the effective date.
p47: (2) Parliament shall enact legislation providing for the classes of
p47: transactions subject to ratification under clause (1).
p47: Legislation relating to the environment.
p47: 72. Parliament shall enact legislation to give full effect to the
p47: provisions of this Part.
p47: CHAPTER SIX—LEADERSHIP AND INTEGRITY
p47: Responsibilities of leadership.
p47: 73. (1) Authority assigned to a State officer—
p47: (a) is a public trust to be exercised in a manner that—
p47: (i) is consistent with the purposes and objects of this
p47: Constitution;
p47: (ii) demonstrates respect for the people;
p47: (iii) brings honour to the nation and dignity to the office; and
p47: (iv) promotes public confidence in the integrity of the office;
p47: and
p47: (b) vests in the State officer the responsibility to serve the people,
p47: rather than the power to rule them.
p47: (2) The guiding principles of leadership and integrity include—
p47: (a) selection on the basis of personal integrity, competence and
p47: suitability, or election in free and fair elections;
p47: (b) objectivity and impartiality in decision making, and in ensuring
p47: that decisions are not influenced by nepotism, favouritism,
p47: other improper motives or corrupt practices;
p47: (c) selfless service based solely on the public interest,
p47: demonstrated by—
p47: (i) honesty in the execution of public duties; and
p47: (ii) the declaration of any personal interest that may conflict
p47: with public duties;
p47: (d) accountability to the public for decisions and actions; and
p47: (e) discipline and commitment in service to the people.
p48: Oath of office of State officers.
p48: 74. Before assuming a State office, acting in a State office, or
p48: performing any functions of a State office, a person shall take and
p48: subscribe the oath or affirmation of office, in the manner and form
p48: prescribed by the Third Schedule or under an Act of Parliament.
p48: Conduct of State officers.
p48: 75. (1) A State officer shall behave, whether in public and official
p48: life, in private life, or in association with other persons, in a manner
p48: that avoids—
p48: (a) any conflict between personal interests and public or official
p48: duties;
p48: (b) compromising any public or official interest in favour of a
p48: personal interest; or
p48: (c) demeaning the office the officer holds.
p48: (2) A person who contravenes clause (1), or Article 76, 77 or 78
p48: (2)—
p48: (a) shall be subject to the applicable disciplinary procedure for
p48: the relevant office; and
p48: (b) may, in accordance with the disciplinary procedure referred to
p48: in paragraph (a), be dismissed or otherwise removed from
p48: office.
p48: (3) A person who has been dismissed or otherwise removed from
p48: office for a contravention of the provisions specified in clause (2) is
p48: disqualified from holding any other State office.
p48: Financial probity of State officers.
p48: 76. (1) A gift or donation to a State officer on a public or official
p48: occasion is a gift or donation to the Republic and shall be delivered to
p48: the State unless exempted under an Act of Parliament.
p48: (2) A State officer shall not—
p48: (a) maintain a bank account outside Kenya except in accordance
p48: with an Act of Parliament; or
p48: (b) seek or accept a personal loan or benefit in circumstances
p48: that compromise the integrity of the State officer.
p48: Restriction on activities of State officers.
p48: 77. (1) A full-time State officer shall not participate in any other
p49: gainful employment.
p49: (2) Any appointed State officer shall not hold office in a political
p49: party.
p49: (3) A retired State officer who is receiving a pension from public
p49: funds shall not hold more than two concurrent remunerative positions
p49: as chairperson, director or employee of—
p49: (a) a company owned or controlled by the State; or
p49: (b) a State organ.
p49: (4) A retired State officer shall not receive remuneration from
p49: public funds other than as contemplated in clause (3).
p49: Citizenship and leadership.
p49: 78. (1) A person is not eligible for election or appointment to a
p49: State office unless the person is a citizen of Kenya.
p49: (2) A State officer or a member of the defence forces shall not
p49: hold dual citizenship.
p49: (3) Clauses (1) and (2) do not apply to—
p49: (a) judges and members of commissions; or
p49: (b) any person who has been made a citizen of another country
p49: by operation of that country’s law, without ability to opt out.
p49: Legislation to establish the ethics and anti-corruption
p49: commission.
p49: 79. Parliament shall enact legislation to establish an independent
p49: ethics and anti-corruption commission, which shall be and have the

p50: CHAPTER SEVEN—REPRESENTATION OF THE PEOPLE
p50: PART 1—ELECTORAL SYSTEM AND PROCESS
p50: General principles for the electoral system.
p50: 81. The electoral system shall comply with the following
p50: principles—
p50: (a) freedom of citizens to exercise their political rights under
p50: Article 38;
p50: (b) not more than two-thirds of the members of elective public
p50: bodies shall be of the same gender;
p50: (c) fair representation of persons with disabilities;
p50: (d) universal suffrage based on the aspiration for fair
p50: representation and equality of vote; and
p50: (e) free and fair elections, which are—
p50: (i) by secret ballot;
p50: (ii) free from violence, intimidation, improper influence or
p50: corruption;
p50: (iii) conducted by an independent body;
p50: (iv) transparent; and
p50: (v) administered in an impartial, neutral, efficient, accurate
p50: and accountable manner.
p50: Legislation on elections.
p50: 82. (1) Parliament shall enact legislation to provide for—
p50: (a) the delimitation by the Independent Electoral and Boundaries
p50: Commission of electoral units for election of members of the
p50: National Assembly and county assemblies;
p50: (b) the nomination of candidates;
p50: (c) the continuous registration of citizens as voters;
p51: (d) the conduct of elections and referenda and the regulation and
p51: efficient supervision of elections and referenda, including the
p51: nomination of candidates for elections; and
p51: (e) the progressive registration of citizens residing outside Kenya,
p51: and the progressive realisation of their right to vote.
p51: (2) Legislation required by clause (1) (d) shall ensure that voting
p51: at every election is—
p51: (a) simple;
p51: (b) transparent; and
p51: (c) takes into account the special needs of—
p51: (i) persons with disabilities; and
p51: (ii) other persons or groups with special needs.
p51: Registration as a voter.
p51: 83. (1) A person qualifies for registration as a voter at elections or
p51: referenda if the person—
p51: (a) is an adult citizen;
p51: (b) is not declared to be of unsound mind; and
p51: (c) has not been convicted of an election offence during the
p51: preceding five years.
p51: (2) A citizen who qualifies for registration as a voter shall be
p51: registered at only one registration centre.
p51: (3) Administrative arrangements for the registration of voters and
p51: the conduct of elections shall be designed to facilitate, and shall not
p51: deny, an eligible citizen the right to vote or stand for election.
p51: Candidates for election and political parties to comply with code
p51: of conduct.
p51: 84. In every election, all candidates and all political parties shall
p51: comply with the code of conduct prescribed by the Independent
p51: Electoral and Boundaries Commission.
p51: Eligibility to stand as an independent candidate.
p51: 85. Any person is eligible to stand as an independent candidate
p51: for election if the person—
p51: (a) is not a member of a registered political party and has not
p52: been a member for at least three months immediately before
p52: the date of the election; and
p52: (b) satisfies the requirements of—
p52: (i) Article 99 (1) (c) (i) or (ii), in the case of a candidate for
p52: election to the National Assembly or the Senate,
p52: respectively; or
p52: (ii) Article 193 (1) (c) (ii), in the case of a candidate for
p52: election to a county assembly.
p52: Voting.
p52: 86. At every election, the Independent Electoral and Boundaries
p52: Commission shall ensure that—
p52: (a) whatever voting method is used, the system is simple,
p52: accurate, verifiable, secure, accountable and transparent;
p52: (b) the votes cast are counted, tabulated and the results
p52: announced promptly by the presiding officer at each polling
p52: station;
p52: (c) the results from the polling stations are openly and accurately
p52: collated and promptly announced by the returning officer; and
p52: (d) appropriate structures and mechanisms to eliminate electoral
p52: malpractice are put in place, including the safekeeping of
p52: election materials.
p52: Electoral disputes.
p52: 87. (1) Parliament shall enact legislation to establish mechanisms
p52: for timely settling of electoral disputes.
p52: (2) Petitions concerning an election, other than a presidential
p52: election, shall be filed within twenty-eight days after the declaration of
p52: the election results by the Independent Electoral and Boundaries
p52: Commission.
p52: (3) Service of a petition may be direct or by advertisement in a
p52: newspaper with national circulation.

p53: (2) A person is not eligible for appointment as a member of the

p55: Allocation of party list seats.

p57: CHAPTER EIGHT—THE LEGISLATURE
p57: PART 1—ESTABLISHMENT AND ROLE OF PARLIAMENT
p57: Establishment of Parliament.
p57: 93. (1) There is established a Parliament of Kenya, which shall
p57: consist of the National Assembly and the Senate.
p57: (2) The National Assembly and the Senate shall perform their
p57: respective functions in accordance with this Constitution.
p57: Role of Parliament.
p57: 94. (1) The legislative authority of the Republic is derived from
p57: the people and, at the national level, is vested in and exercised by
p57: Parliament.
p58: (2) Parliament manifests the diversity of the nation, represents
p58: the will of the people, and exercises their sovereignty.
p58: (3) Parliament may consider and pass amendments to this
p58: Constitution, and alter county boundaries as provided for in this
p58: Constitution.
p58: (4) Parliament shall protect this Constitution and promote the
p58: democratic governance of the Republic.
p58: (5) No person or body, other than Parliament, has the power to
p58: make provision having the force of law in Kenya except under authority
p58: conferred by this Constitution or by legislation.
p58: (6) An Act of Parliament, or legislation of a county, that confers
p58: on any State organ, State officer or person the authority to make
p58: provision having the force of law in Kenya, as contemplated in clause
p58: (5), shall expressly specify the purpose and objectives for which that
p58: authority is conferred, the limits of the authority, the nature and scope
p58: of the law that may be made, and the principles and standards
p58: applicable to the law made under the authority.
p58: Role of the National Assembly.
p58: 95. (1) The National Assembly represents the people of the
p58: constituencies and special interests in the National Assembly.
p58: (2) The National Assembly deliberates on and resolves issues of
p58: concern to the people.
p58: (3) The National Assembly enacts legislation in accordance with
p58: Part 4 of this Chapter.
p58: (4) The National Assembly—
p58: (a) determines the allocation of national revenue between the
p58: levels of government, as provided in Part 4 of Chapter
p58: Twelve;
p58: (b) appropriates funds for expenditure by the national
p58: government and other national State organs; and
p58: (c) exercises oversight over national revenue and its expenditure.
p58: (5) The National Assembly—
p58: (a) reviews the conduct in office of the President, the Deputy
p58: President and other State officers and initiates the process of
p58: removing them from office; and
p59: (b) exercises oversight of State organs.
p59: (6) The National Assembly approves declarations of war and
p59: extensions of states of emergency.
p59: Role of the Senate.
p59: 96. (1) The Senate represents the counties, and serves to protect
p59: the interests of the counties and their governments.
p59: (2) The Senate participates in the law-making function of
p59: Parliament by considering, debating and approving Bills concerning
p59: counties, as provided in Articles 109 to 113.
p59: (3) The Senate determines the allocation of national revenue
p59: among counties, as provided in Article 217, and exercises oversight
p59: over national revenue allocated to the county governments.
p59: (4) The Senate participates in the oversight of State officers by
p59: considering and determining any resolution to remove the President or
p59: Deputy President from office in accordance with Article 145.

p59: (d) the Speaker, who is an ex officio member.

p59: person from contesting an election under clause (1) (a).
p59: Membership of the Senate.
p59: 98. (1) The Senate consists of—
p59: (a) forty-seven members each elected by the registered voters of
p60: the counties, each county constituting a single member
p60: constituency;

p62: Constitution or legislation enacted under Article 80;
p63: (d) if the member resigns from Parliament in writing to the

p65: (4) A Bill concerning county government may originate in the
p65: National Assembly or the Senate, and is passed in accordance with
p65: Articles 110 to 113, Articles 122 and 123 and the Standing Orders of
p65: the Houses.
p65: (5) A Bill may be introduced by any member or committee of the
p65: relevant House of Parliament, but a money Bill may be introduced only
p65: in the National Assembly in accordance with Article 114.
p65: Bills concerning county government.
p65: 110. (1) In this Constitution, “a Bill concerning county
p65: government” means—
p65: (a) a Bill containing provisions affecting the functions and powers
p65: of the county governments set out in the Fourth Schedule;
p65: (b) a Bill relating to the election of members of a county assembly
p65: or a county executive; and
p66: (c) a Bill referred to in Chapter Twelve affecting the finances of
p66: county governments.
p66: (2) A Bill concerning county governments is—
p66: (a) a special Bill, which shall be considered under Article 111, if it
p66: —
p66: (i) relates to the election of members of a county assembly or
p66: a county executive; or
p66: (ii) is the annual County Allocation of Revenue Bill referred to
p66: in Article 218; or
p66: (b) an ordinary Bill, which shall be considered under Article 112,
p66: in any other case.
p66: (3) Before either House considers a Bill, the Speakers of the
p66: National Assembly and Senate shall jointly resolve any question as to
p66: whether it is a Bill concerning counties and, if it is, whether it is a
p66: special or an ordinary Bill.
p66: (4) When any Bill concerning county government has been
p66: passed by one House of Parliament, the Speaker of that House shall
p66: refer it to the Speaker of the other House.
p66: (5) If both Houses pass the Bill in the same form, the Speaker of
p66: the House in which the Bill originated shall, within seven days, refer
p66: the Bill to the President for assent.
p66: Special Bills concerning county governments.
p66: 111. (1) A special Bill concerning a county government shall
p66: proceed in the same manner as an ordinary Bill concerning county
p66: government, subject to clauses (2) and (3).
p66: (2) The National Assembly may amend or veto a special Bill that
p66: has been passed by the Senate only by a resolution supported by at
p66: least two-thirds of the members of the Assembly.
p66: (3) If a resolution in the National Assembly to amend or veto a
p66: special Bill fails to pass, the Speaker of the Assembly shall, within
p66: seven days, refer the Bill, in the form adopted by the Senate, to the
p66: President for assent.
p66: Ordinary Bills concerning county governments.
p66: 112. (1) If one House passes an ordinary Bill concerning
p66: counties, and the second House—
p67: (a) rejects the Bill, it shall be referred to a mediation committee
p67: appointed under Article 113; or
p67: (b) passes the Bill in an amended form, it shall be referred back
p67: to the originating House for reconsideration.
p67: (2) If, after the originating House has reconsidered a Bill referred
p67: back to it under clause (1) (b), that House—
p67: (a) passes the Bill as amended, the Speaker of that House shall
p67: refer the Bill to the President within seven days for assent; or
p67: (b) rejects the Bill as amended, the Bill shall be referred to a
p67: mediation committee under Article 113.
p67: Mediation committees.
p67: 113. (1) If a Bill is referred to a mediation committee under Article
p67: 112, the Speakers of both Houses shall appoint a mediation committee
p67: consisting of equal numbers of members of each House to attempt to
p67: develop a version of the Bill that both Houses will pass.
p67: (2) If the mediation committee agrees on a version of the Bill,
p67: each House shall vote to approve or reject that version of the Bill.
p67: (3) If both Houses approve the version of the Bill proposed by the
p67: mediation committee, the Speaker of the National Assembly shall refer
p67: the Bill to the President within seven days for assent.
p67: (4) If the mediation committee fails to agree on a version of the
p67: Bill within thirty days, or if a version proposed by the committee is
p67: rejected by either House, the Bill is defeated.
p67: Money Bills.
p67: 114. (1) A money Bill may not deal with any matter other than
p67: those listed in the definition of “ a money Bill” in clause (3).
p67: (2) If, in the opinion of the Speaker of the National Assembly, a
p67: motion makes provision for a matter listed in the definition of “a money
p67: Bill”, the Assembly may proceed only in accordance with the
p67: recommendation of the relevant Committee of the Assembly after
p67: taking into account the views of the Cabinet Secretary responsible for
p67: finance.
p67: (3) In this Constitution, “a money Bill” means a Bill, other than a
p67: Bill specified in Article 218, that contains provisions dealing with—
p67: (a) taxes;
p68: (b) the imposition of charges on a public fund or the variation or
p68: repeal of any of those charges;
p68: (c) the appropriation, receipt, custody, investment or issue of
p68: public money;
p68: (d) the raising or guaranteeing of any loan or its repayment; or
p68: (e) matters incidental to any of those matters.
p68: (4) In clause (3), “tax”, “public money”, and “loan” do not include
p68: any tax, public money or loan raised by a county.
p68: Presidential assent and referral.
p68: 115. (1) Within fourteen days after receipt of a Bill, the President
p68: shall—
p68: (a) assent to the Bill; or
p68: (b) refer the Bill back to Parliament for reconsideration by
p68: Parliament, noting any reservations that the President has
p68: concerning the Bill.
p68: (2) If the President refers a Bill back for reconsideration,
p68: Parliament may, following the appropriate procedures under this Part
p68: —
p68: (a) amend the Bill in light of the President’s reservations; or
p68: (b) pass the Bill a second time without amendment.
p68: (3) If Parliament amendeds the Bill fully accommodating the
p68: President’s reservations, the appropriate Speaker shall re-submit it to
p68: the President for assent.
p68: (4) Parliament, after considering the President’s reservations,
p68: may pass the Bill a second time, without amendment, or with
p68: amendments that do not fully accommodate the President’s
p68: reservations, by a vote supported—
p68: (a) by two-thirds of members of the National Assembly; and
p68: (b) two-thirds of the delegations in the Senate, if it is a Bill that
p68: requires the approval of the Senate.
p68: (5) If Parliament has passed a Bill under clause (4)—
p68: (a) the appropriate Speaker shall within seven days re-submit it
p68: to the President; and
p69: (b) the President shall within seven days assent to the Bill.
p69: (6) If the President does not assent to a Bill or refer it back within
p69: the period prescribed in clause (1), or assent to it under (5) (b), the Bill
p69: shall be taken to have been assented to on the expiry of that period.
p69: Coming into force of laws.
p69: 116. (1) A Bill passed by Parliament and assented to by the
p69: President shall be published in the Gazette as an Act of Parliament
p69: within seven days after assent.
p69: (2) Subject to clause (3), an Act of Parliament comes into force
p69: on the fourteenth day after its publication in the Gazette, unless the Act
p69: stipulates a different date on or time at which it will come into force.
p69: (3) An Act of Parliament that confers a direct pecuniary interest
p69: on members of Parliament shall not come into force until after the next
p69: general election of members of Parliament.
p69: (4) Clause (3) does not apply to an interest that members of
p69: Parliament have as members of the public.
p69: PART 5—PARLIAMENT’S GENERAL PROCEDURES AND RULES
p69: Powers, privileges and immunities.
p69: 117. (1) There shall be freedom of speech and debate in
p69: Parliament.
p69: (2) Parliament may, for the purpose of the orderly and effective
p69: discharge of the business of Parliament, provide for the powers,
p69: privileges and immunities of Parliament, its committees, the leader of
p69: the majority party, the leader of the minority party, the chairpersons of
p69: committees and members.
p69: Public access and participation.
p69: 118. (1) Parliament shall—
p69: (a) conduct its business in an open manner, and its sittings and
p69: those of its committees shall be open to the public; and
p69: (b) facilitate public participation and involvement in the legislative
p69: and other business of Parliament and its committees.
p69: (2) Parliament may not exclude the public, or any media, from
p69: any sitting unless in exceptional circumstances the relevant Speaker
p69: has determined that there are justifiable reasons for the exclusion.
p70: Right to petition Parliament.
p70: 119. (1) Every person has a right to petition Parliament to
p70: consider any matter within its authority, including to enact, amend or
p70: repeal any legislation.
p70: (2) Parliament shall make provision for the procedure for the
p70: exercise of this right.
p70: Official languages of Parliament.
p70: 120. (1) The official languages of Parliament shall be Kiswahili,
p70: English and Kenyan Sign language, and the business of Parliament
p70: may be conducted in English, Kiswahili and Kenyan Sign language.
p70: (2) In case of a conflict between different language versions of an
p70: Act of Parliament, the version signed by the President shall prevail.
p70: Quorum.
p70: 121. The quorum of Parliament shall be––
p70: (a) fifty members, in the case of the National Assembly; or
p70: (b) fifteen members, in the case of the Senate.
p70: Voting in Parliament.
p70: 122. (1) Except as otherwise provided in this Constitution, any
p70: question proposed for decision in either House of Parliament shall be
p70: determined by a majority of the members in that House, present and
p70: voting.
p70: (2) On a question proposed for decision in either House—
p70: (a) the Speaker has no vote; and
p70: (b) in the case of a tie, the question is lost.
p70: (3) A member shall not vote on any question in which the
p70: member has a pecuniary interest.
p70: (4) In reckoning the number of members of a House of
p70: Parliament for any purpose of voting in that House, the Speaker of that
p70: House shall not be counted as a member.
p70: Decisions of Senate.
p70: 123. (1) On election, all the members of the Senate who were
p70: registered as voters in a particular county shall collectively constitute a
p70: single delegation for purposes of clause (4) and the member elected
p70: under Article 98 (1) (a) shall be the head of the delegation.
p71: (2) When the Senate is to vote on any matter other than a Bill,
p71: the Speaker shall rule on whether the matter affects or does not affect
p71: counties.
p71: (3) When the Senate votes on a matter that does not affect
p71: counties, each senator has one vote.
p71: (4) Except as provided otherwise in this Constitution, in any
p71: matter in the Senate affecting counties—
p71: (a) each county delegation shall have one vote to be cast on
p71: behalf of the county by the head of the county delegation or,
p71: in the absence of the head of the delegation, by another
p71: member of the delegation designated by the head of the
p71: delegation;
p71: (b) the person who votes on behalf of a delegation shall
p71: determine whether or not to vote in support of, or against, the
p71: matter, after consulting the other members of the delegation;
p71: and
p71: (c) the matter is carried only if it is supported by a majority of all
p71: the delegations.
p71: Committees and Standing Orders.
p71: 124. (1) Each House of Parliament may establish committees,
p71: and shall make Standing Orders for the orderly conduct of its
p71: proceedings, including the proceedings of its committees.
p71: (2) Parliament may establish joint committees consisting of
p71: members of both Houses and may jointly regulate the procedure of
p71: those committees.
p71: (3) The proceedings of either House are not invalid just because
p71: of—
p71: (a) a vacancy in its membership; or
p71: (b) the presence or participation of any person not entitled to be
p71: present at, or to participate in, the proceedings of the House.
p71: (4) When a House of Parliament considers any appointment for
p71: which its approval is required under this Constitution or an Act of
p71: Parliament—
p71: (a) the appointment shall be considered by a committee of the
p71: relevant House;
p72: (b) the committee’s recommendation shall be tabled in the House
p72: for approval; and
p72: (c) the proceedings of the committee and the House shall be
p72: open to the public.
p72: Power to call for evidence.
p72: 125. (1) Either House of Parliament, and any of its committees,
p72: has power to summon any person to appear before it for the purpose
p72: of giving evidence or providing information.
p72: (2) For the purposes of clause (1), a House of Parliament and
p72: any of its committees has the same powers as the High Court—
p72: (a) to enforce the attendance of witnesses and examine them on
p72: oath, affirmation or otherwise;
p72: (b) to compel the production of documents; and
p72: (c) to issue a commission or request to examine witnesses
p72: abroad.

p74: CHAPTER NINE—THE EXECUTIVE
p74: PART 1—PRINCIPLES AND STRUCTURE OF THE NATIONAL EXECUTIVE
p74: Principles of executive authority.
p74: 129. (1) Executive authority derives from the people of Kenya
p74: and shall be exercised in accordance with this Constitution.
p74: (2) Executive authority shall be exercised in a manner compatible
p74: with the principle of service to the people of Kenya, and for their
p74: well-being and benefit.
p74: The National Executive.
p74: 130. (1) The national executive of the Republic comprises the
p74: President, the Deputy President and the rest of the Cabinet.
p74: (2) The composition of the national executive shall reflect the
p74: regional and ethnic diversity of the people of Kenya.
p74: PART 2—THE PRESIDENT AND DEPUTY PRESIDENT
p74: Authority of the President.
p74: 131. (1) The President—
p74: (a) is the Head of State and Government;
p74: (b) exercises the executive authority of the Republic, with the
p74: assistance of the Deputy President and Cabinet Secretaries;
p74: (c) is the Commander-in-Chief of the Kenya Defence Forces;
p74: (d) is the chairperson of the National Security Council; and
p74: (e) is a symbol of national unity.
p75: (2) The President shall—
p75: (a) respect, uphold and safeguard this Constitution;
p75: (b) safeguard the sovereignty of the Republic;
p75: (c) promote and enhance the unity of the nation;
p75: (d) promote respect for the diversity of the people and
p75: communities of Kenya; and
p75: (e) ensure the protection of human rights and fundamental
p75: freedoms and the rule of law.
p75: (3) The President shall not hold any other State or public office.
p75: Functions of the President.
p75: 132. (1) The President shall—
p75: (a) address the opening of each newly elected Parliament;
p75: (b) address a special sitting of Parliament once every year and
p75: may address Parliament at any other time; and
p75: (c) once every year—
p75: (i) report, in an address to the nation, on all the measures
p75: taken and the progress achieved in the realisation of
p75: the national values, referred to in Article 10;
p75: (ii) publish in the Gazette the details of the measures and
p75: progress under sub-paragraph (i); and
p75: (iii) submit a report for debate to the National Assembly on
p75: the progress made in fulfilling the international
p75: obligations of the Republic.
p75: (2) The President shall nominate and, with the approval of the
p75: National Assembly, appoint, and may dismiss—
p75: (a) the Cabinet Secretaries, in accordance with Article 152;
p75: (b) the Attorney-General, in accordance with Article 156;
p75: (c) the Secretary to the Cabinet in accordance with Article 154;
p75: (d) Principal Secretaries in accordance with Article 155;
p75: (e) high commissioners, ambassadors and diplomatic and
p75: consular representatives; and
p76: (f) in accordance with this Constitution, any other State or public
p76: officer whom this Constitution requires or empowers the
p76: President to appoint or dismiss.
p76: (3) The President shall—
p76: (a) chair Cabinet meetings;
p76: (b) direct and co-ordinate the functions of ministries and
p76: government departments; and
p76: (c) by a decision published in the Gazette, assign responsibility
p76: for the implementation and administration of any Act of
p76: Parliament to a Cabinet Secretary, to the extent not
p76: inconsistent with any Act of Parliament.
p76: (4) The President may—
p76: (a) perform any other executive function provided for in this
p76: Constitution or in national legislation and, except as otherwise
p76: provided for in this Constitution, may establish an office in the
p76: public service in accordance with the recommendation of the
p76: Public Service Commission;
p76: (b) receive foreign diplomatic and consular representatives;
p76: (c) confer honours in the name of the people and the Republic;
p76: (d) subject to Article 58, declare a state of emergency; and
p76: (e) with the approval of Parliament, declare war.
p76: (5) The President shall ensure that the international obligations of
p76: the Republic are fulfilled through the actions of the relevant Cabinet
p76: Secretaries.
p76: Power of mercy.
p76: 133. (1) On the petition of any person, the President may
p76: exercise a power of mercy in accordance with the advice of the
p76: Advisory Committee established under clause (2), by—
p76: (a) granting a free or conditional pardon to a person convicted of
p76: an offence;
p76: (b) postponing the carrying out of a punishment, either for a
p76: specified or indefinite period;
p76: (c) substituting a less severe form of punishment; or
p77: (d) remitting all or part of a punishment.
p77: (2) There shall be an Advisory Committee on the Power of Mercy,
p77: comprising—
p77: (a) the Attorney-General;
p77: (b) the Cabinet Secretary responsible for correctional services;
p77: and
p77: (c) at least five other members as prescribed by an Act of
p77: Parliament, none of whom may be a State officer or in public
p77: service.
p77: (3) Parliament shall enact legislation to provide for—
p77: (a) the tenure of the members of the Advisory Committee;
p77: (b) the procedure of the Advisory Committee; and
p77: (c) criteria that shall be applied by the Advisory Committee in
p77: formulating its advice.
p77: (4) The Advisory Committee may take into account the views of
p77: the victims of the offence in respect of which it is considering making
p77: recommendations to the President.
p77: Exercise of presidential powers during temporary incumbency.
p77: 134. (1) A person who holds the office of President or who is
p77: authorised in terms of this Constitution to exercise the powers of the
p77: President—
p77: (a) during the period commencing on the date of the first vote in a
p77: presidential election, and ending when the newly elected
p77: President assumes office; or
p77: (b) while the President is absent or incapacitated, or at other
p77: times contemplated in Article 147 (3),
p77: may not exercise the powers of the President specified in clause (2).
p77: (2) The powers referred to in clause (1) are—
p77: (a) the nomination or appointment of the judges of the superior
p77: courts;
p77: (b) the nomination or appointment of any other public officer
p77: whom this Constitution or legislation requires the President to
p77: appoint;
p78: (c) the nomination or appointment or dismissal of Cabinet
p78: Secretaries and other State or Public officers;
p78: (d) the nomination or appointment or dismissal of a high
p78: commissioner, ambassador, or diplomatic or consular
p78: representative;
p78: (e) the power of mercy; and
p78: (f) the authority to confer honours in the name of the people and
p78: the Republic.
p78: Decisions of the President.
p78: 135. A decision of the President in the performance of any
p78: function of the President under this Constitution shall be in writing and
p78: shall bear the seal and signature of the President.
p78: Election of the President.
p78: 136. (1) The President shall be elected by registered voters in a
p78: national election conducted in accordance with this Constitution and
p78: any Act of Parliament regulating presidential elections.
p78: (2) An election of the President shall be held—
p78: (a) on the same day as a general election of Members of
p78: Parliament, being the second Tuesday in August, in every
p78: fifth year; or
p78: (b) in the circumstances contemplated in Article 146.
p78: Qualifications and disqualifications for election as President.
p78: 137. (1) A person qualifies for nomination as a presidential
p78: candidate if the person—
p78: (a) is a citizen by birth;
p78: (b) is qualified to stand for election as a member of Parliament;
p78: (c) is nominated by a political party, or is an independent
p78: candidate; and
p78: (d) is nominated by not fewer than two thousand voters from
p78: each of a majority of the counties.
p78: (2) A person is not qualified for nomination as a presidential
p78: candidate if the person—
p78: (a) owes allegiance to a foreign state; or
p79: (b) is a public officer, or is acting in any State or other public
p79: office.
p79: (3) Clause (2) (b) shall not apply to—
p79: (a) the President;
p79: (b) the Deputy President; or
p79: (c) a member of Parliament.
p79: Procedure at presidential election.
p79: 138. (1) If only one candidate for President is nominated, that
p79: candidate shall be declared elected.
p79: (2) If two or more candidates for President are nominated, an
p79: election shall be held in each constituency.
p79: (3) In a presidential election—
p79: (a) all persons registered as voters for the purposes of
p79: parliamentary elections are entitled to vote;
p79: (b) the poll shall be taken by secret ballot on the day specified in
p79: Article 101 (1) at the time, in the places and in the manner
p79: prescribed under an Act of Parliament; and
p79: (c) after counting the votes in the polling stations, the
p79: Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission shall tally
p79: and verify the count and declare the result.
p79: (4) A candidate shall be declared elected as President if the
p79: candidate receives—
p79: (a) more than half of all the votes cast in the election; and
p79: (b) at least twenty-five per cent of the votes cast in each of more
p79: than half of the counties.
p79: (5) If no candidate is elected, a fresh election shall be held within
p79: thirty days after the previous election and in that fresh election the only
p79: candidates shall be—
p79: (a) the candidate, or the candidates, who received the greatest
p79: number of votes; and
p79: (b) the candidate, or the candidates, who received the second
p79: greatest number of votes.
p80: (6) If more than one candidate receives the greatest number of
p80: votes, clause (5) (b) shall not apply and the only candidates in the
p80: fresh election shall be those contemplated in clause (5) (a).
p80: (7) The candidate who receives the most votes in the fresh
p80: election shall be declared elected as President.
p80: (8) A presidential election shall be cancelled and a new election
p80: held if—
p80: (a) no person has been nominated as a candidate before the
p80: expiry of the period set for the delivery of nominations;
p80: (b) a candidate for election as President or Deputy President dies
p80: on or before the scheduled election date; or
p80: (c) a candidate who would have been entitled to be declared
p80: elected as President, dies before being declared elected as
p80: President.
p80: (9) A new presidential election under clause (8) shall be held
p80: within sixty days after the date set for the previous presidential
p80: election.
p80: (10) Within seven days after the presidential election, the
p80: chairperson of the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission
p80: shall—
p80: (a) declare the result of the election; and
p80: (b) deliver a written notification of the result to the Chief Justice
p80: and the incumbent President.
p80: Death before assuming office.
p80: 139. (1) If a President-elect dies after being declared elected as
p80: President, but before assuming office—
p80: (a) the Deputy President-elect shall be sworn in as acting
p80: President on the date on which the President-elect would
p80: otherwise have been sworn-in; and
p80: (b) a fresh election to the office of President shall be held within
p80: sixty days after the death of the President-elect.
p80: (2) If the Deputy President-elect dies before assuming office, the
p80: office of the Deputy President shall be declared vacant on the
p80: assumption of office by the person declared elected as the President.
p80: (3) If both the persons declared elected as the President and the
p81: Deputy President die before assuming office—
p81: (a) the Speaker of the National Assembly shall act as President
p81: from the date on which the President-elect would otherwise
p81: have been sworn-in; and
p81: (b) a fresh presidential election shall be conducted within sixty
p81: days after the second death.
p81: Questions as to validity of presidential election.
p81: 140. (1) A person may file a petition in the Supreme Court to

p94: CHAPTER TEN—JUDICIARY
p94: PART 1—JUDICIAL AUTHORITY AND LEGAL SYSTEM
p94: Judicial authority.
p94: 159. (1) Judicial authority is derived from the people and vests in,
p94: and shall be exercised by, the courts and tribunals established by or
p94: under this Constitution.
p94: (2) In exercising judicial authority, the courts and tribunals shall
p94: be guided by the following principles—
p94: (a) justice shall be done to all, irrespective of status;
p94: (b) justice shall not be delayed;
p94: (c) alternative forms of dispute resolution including reconciliation,
p95: mediation, arbitration and traditional dispute resolution
p95: mechanisms shall be promoted, subject to clause (3);
p95: (d) justice shall be administered without undue regard to
p95: procedural technicalities; and
p95: (e) the purpose and principles of this Constitution shall be
p95: protected and promoted.
p95: (3) Traditional dispute resolution mechanisms shall not be used
p95: in a way that—
p95: (a) contravenes the Bill of Rights;
p95: (b) is repugnant to justice and morality or results in outcomes that
p95: are repugnant to justice or morality; or
p95: (c) is inconsistent with this Constitution or any written law.
p95: Independence of the Judiciary.
p95: 160. (1) In the exercise of judicial authority, the Judiciary, as
p95: constituted by Article 161, shall be subject only to this Constitution and
p95: the law and shall not be subject to the control or direction of any
p95: person or authority.
p95: (2) The office of a judge of a superior court shall not be abolished
p95: while there is a substantive holder of the office.
p95: (3) The remuneration and benefits payable to or in respect of
p95: judges shall be a charge on the Consolidated Fund.
p95: (4) Subject to Article 168(6), the remuneration and benefits
p95: payable to, or in respect of, a judge shall not be varied to the
p95: disadvantage of that judge, and the retirement benefits of a retired
p95: judge shall not be varied to the disadvantage of the retired judge
p95: during the lifetime of that retired judge.
p95: (5) A member of the Judiciary is not liable in an action or suit in
p95: respect of anything done or omitted to be done in good faith in the
p95: lawful performance of a judicial function.
p95: Judicial offices and officers.
p95: 161. (1) The Judiciary consists of the judges of the superior
p95: courts, magistrates, other judicial officers and staff.
p95: (2) There is established the office of—
p95: (a) Chief Justice, who shall be the Head of the Judiciary;
p96: (b) Deputy Chief Justice, who shall be the Deputy Head of the
p96: Judiciary; and
p96: (c) Chief Registrar of the Judiciary, who shall be the chief
p96: administrator and accounting officer of the Judiciary.
p96: (3) The Judicial Service Commission may establish other offices
p96: of registrar as may be necessary.
p96: System of courts.
p96: 162. (1) The superior courts are the Supreme Court, the Court of
p96: Appeal, the High Court and the courts referred to in clause (2).
p96: (2) Parliament shall establish courts with the status of the High
p96: Court to hear and determine disputes relating to—
p96: (a) employment and labour relations; and
p96: (b) the environment and the use and occupation of, and title to,
p96: land.
p96: (3) Parliament shall determine the jurisdiction and functions of the
p96: courts contemplated in clause (2).
p96: (4) The subordinate courts are the courts established under
p96: Article 169, or by Parliament in accordance with that Article.
p96: PART 2—SUPERIOR COURTS
p96: Supreme Court.
p96: 163. (1) There is established the Supreme Court, which shall
p96: consists of—
p96: (a) the Chief Justice, who shall be the president of the court;
p96: (b) the Deputy Chief Justice, who shall—
p96: (i) deputise for the Chief Justice; and
p96: (ii) be the vice-president of the court; and
p96: (c) five other judges.
p96: (2) The Supreme Court shall be properly constituted for the
p96: purposes of its proceedings if it is composed of five judges.
p96: (3) The Supreme Court shall have—
p96: (a) exclusive original jurisdiction to hear and determine disputes
p97: relating to the elections to the office of President arising
p97: under Article 140; and
p97: (b) subject to clause (4) and (5), appellate jurisdiction to hear and
p97: determine appeals from—
p97: (i) the Court of Appeal; and
p97: (ii) any other court or tribunal as prescribed by national
p97: legislation.
p97: (4) Appeals shall lie from the Court of Appeal to the Supreme
p97: Court—
p97: (a) as of right in any case involving the interpretation or
p97: application of this Constitution; and
p97: (b) in any other case in which the Supreme Court, or the Court of
p97: Appeal, certifies that a matter of general public importance is
p97: involved, subject to clause (5).
p97: (5) A certification by the Court of Appeal under clause (4) (b) may
p97: be reviewed by the Supreme Court, and either affirmed, varied or
p97: overturned.
p97: (6) The Supreme Court may give an advisory opinion at the
p97: request of the national government, any State organ, or any county
p97: government with respect to any matter concerning county government.
p97: (7) All courts, other than the Supreme Court, are bound by the
p97: decisions of the Supreme Court.
p97: (8) The Supreme Court shall make rules for the exercise of its
p97: jurisdiction.
p97: (9) An Act of Parliament may make further provision for the
p97: operation of the Supreme Court.
p97: Court of Appeal.
p97: 164. (1) There is established the Court of Appeal, which—
p97: (a) shall consist of the number of judges, being not fewer than
p97: twelve, as may be prescribed by an Act of Parliament; and
p97: (b) shall be organised and administered in the manner prescribed
p97: by an Act of Parliament.
p97: (2) There shall be a president of the Court of Appeal who shall be
p97: elected by the judges of the Court of Appeal from among themselves.
p98: (3) The Court of Appeal has jurisdiction to hear appeals from—
p98: (a) the High Court; and
p98: (b) any other court or tribunal as prescribed by an Act of
p98: Parliament.
p98: High Court.
p98: 165. (1) There is established the High Court, which—
p98: (a) shall consist of the number of judges prescribed by an Act of
p98: Parliament; and
p98: (b) shall be organised and administered in the manner prescribed
p98: by an Act of Parliament.
p98: (2) There shall be a Principal Judge of the High Court, who shall
p98: be elected by the judges of the High Court from among themselves.
p98: (3) Subject to clause (5), the High Court shall have—
p98: (a) unlimited original jurisdiction in criminal and civil matters;
p98: (b) jurisdiction to determine the question whether a right or
p98: fundamental freedom in the Bill of Rights has been denied,
p98: violated, infringed or threatened;
p98: (c) jurisdiction to hear an appeal from a decision of a tribunal
p98: appointed under this Constitution to consider the removal of a
p98: person from office, other than a tribunal appointed under
p98: Article 144;
p98: (d) jurisdiction to hear any question respecting the interpretation
p98: of this Constitution including the determination of—
p98: (i) the question whether any law is inconsistent with or in
p98: contravention of this Constitution;
p98: (ii) the question whether anything said to be done under the
p98: authority of this Constitution or of any law is
p98: inconsistent with, or in contravention of, this
p98: Constitution;
p98: (iii) any matter relating to constitutional powers of State
p98: organs in respect of county governments and any
p98: matter relating to the constitutional relationship
p98: between the levels of government; and
p98: (iv) a question relating to conflict of laws under Article 191;
p98: and
p99: (e) any other jurisdiction, original or appellate, conferred on it by
p99: legislation.
p99: (4) Any matter certified by the court as raising a substantial
p99: question of law under clause (3) (b) or (d) shall be heard by an uneven
p99: number of judges, being not less than three, assigned by the Chief
p99: Justice.
p99: (5) The High Court shall not have jurisdiction in respect of
p99: matters—
p99: (a) reserved for the exclusive jurisdiction of the Supreme Court
p99: under this Constitution; or
p99: (b) falling within the jurisdiction of the courts contemplated in
p99: Article 162 (2).
p99: (6) The High Court has supervisory jurisdiction over the
p99: subordinate courts and over any person, body or authority exercising a
p99: judicial or quasi-judicial function, but not over a superior court.
p99: (7) For the purposes of clause (6), the High Court may call for the
p99: record of any proceedings before any subordinate court or person,
p99: body or authority referred to in clause (6), and may make any order or
p99: give any direction it considers appropriate to ensure the fair
p99: administration of justice.
p99: Appointment of Chief Justice, Deputy Chief Justice and other
p99: judges.
p99: 166. (1) The President shall appoint—
p99: (a) the Chief Justice and the Deputy Chief Justice, in accordance
p99: with the recommendation of the Judicial Service Commission,
p99: and subject to the approval of the National Assembly; and
p99: (b) all other judges, in accordance with the recommendation of
p99: the Judicial Service Commission.
p99: (2) Each judge of a superior court shall be appointed from among
p99: persons who—
p99: (a) hold a law degree from a recognised university, or are
p99: advocates of the High Court of Kenya, or possess an
p99: equivalent qualification in a common-law jurisdiction;
p99: (b) possess the experience required under clause (3) to (6) as
p99: applicable, irrespective of whether that experience was
p99: gained in Kenya or in another Commonwealth common-law
p99: jurisdiction; and
p100: (c) have a high moral character, integrity and impartiality.
p100: (3) The Chief Justice and other judges of the Supreme Court
p100: shall be appointed from among persons who have—
p100: (a) at least fifteen years experience as a superior court judge; or
p100: (b) at least fifteen years’ experience as a distinguished academic,
p100: judicial officer, legal practitioner or such experience in other
p100: relevant legal field; or
p100: (c) held the qualifications specified in paragraphs (a) and (b) for a
p100: period amounting, in the aggregate, to fifteen years.
p100: (4) Each judge of the Court of Appeal shall be appointed from
p100: among persons who have—
p100: (a) at least ten years’ experience as a superior court judge; or
p100: (b) at least ten years’ experience as a distinguished academic or
p100: legal practitioner or such experience in other relevant legal
p100: field; or
p100: (c) held the qualifications mentioned in paragraphs (a) and (b) for
p100: a period amounting, in the aggregate, to ten years.
p100: (5) Each judge of the High Court shall be appointed from among
p100: persons who have—
p100: (a) at least ten years’ experience as a superior court judge or
p100: professionally qualified magistrate; or
p100: (b) at least ten years’ experience as a distinguished academic or
p100: legal practitioner or such experience in other relevant legal
p100: field; or
p100: (c) held the qualifications specified in paragraphs (a) and (b) for
p100: a period amounting, in the aggregate, to ten years.
p100: Tenure of office of the Chief Justice and other judges.
p100: 167. (1) A judge shall retire from office on attaining the age of
p100: seventy years, but may elect to retire at any time after attaining the
p100: age of sixty-five years.
p100: (2) The Chief Justice shall hold office for a maximum of ten years
p100: or until retiring under clause (1), whichever is the earlier.
p100: (3) If the Chief Justice’s term of office expires before the Chief
p100: Justice retires under clause (1), the Chief Justice may continue in
p101: office as a judge of the Supreme Court.
p101: (4) If, on the expiry of the term of office of a Chief Justice, the
p101: Chief Justice opts to remain on the Supreme Court under clause (3),
p101: the next person appointed as Chief Justice may be selected in
p101: accordance with Article 166 (1), even though that appointment may
p101: result in there being more than the maximum permitted number of
p101: Supreme Court judges holding office.
p101: (5) The Chief Justice and any other judge may resign from office
p101: by giving notice, in writing, to the President.
p101: Removal from office.
p101: 168. (1) A judge of a superior court may be removed from office
p101: only on the grounds of—
p101: (a) inability to perform the functions of office arising from mental
p101: or physical incapacity;
p101: (b) a breach of a code of conduct prescribed for judges of the
p101: superior courts by an Act of Parliament;
p101: (c) bankruptcy;
p101: (d) incompetence; or
p101: (e) gross misconduct or misbehaviour.
p101: (2) The removal of a judge may be initiated only by the Judicial
p101: Service Commission acting on its own motion, or on the petition of any
p101: person to the Judicial Service Commission.
p101: (3) A petition by a person to the Judicial Service Commission
p101: under clause (2) shall be in writing, setting out the alleged facts
p101: constituting the grounds for the judges removal.
p101: (4) The Judicial Service Commission shall consider the petition
p101: and, if it is satisfied that the petition discloses a ground for removal
p101: under clause (1), send the petition to the President.
p101: (5) The President shall, within fourteen days after receiving the
p101: petition, suspend the judge from office and, acting in accordance with
p101: the recommendation of the Judicial Service Commission—
p101: (a) in the case of the Chief Justice, appoint a tribunal consisting
p101: of—
p101: (i) the Speaker of the National Assembly, as chairperson;
p102: (ii) three superior court judges from common-law jurisdictions;
p102: (iii) one advocate of fifteen years standing; and
p102: (iv) two other persons with experience in public affairs; or
p102: (b) in the case of a judge other than the Chief Justice, appoint a
p102: tribunal consisting of—
p102: (i) a chairperson and three other members from among
p102: persons who hold or have held office as a judge of a
p102: superior court, or who are qualified to be appointed as
p102: such but who, in either case, have not been members
p102: of the Judicial Service Commission at any time within
p102: the immediately preceding three years;
p102: (ii) one advocate of fifteen years standing; and
p102: (iii) two other persons with experience in public affairs.
p102: (6) Despite Article 160 (4), the remuneration and benefits payable
p102: to a judge who is suspended from office under clause (5) shall be
p102: adjusted to one half until such time as the judge is removed from, or
p102: reinstated in, office.
p102: (7) A tribunal appointed under clause (5) shall—
p102: (a) be responsible for the regulation of its proceedings, subject to
p102: any legislation contemplated in clause (10); and
p102: (b) inquire into the matter expeditiously and report on the facts
p102: and make binding recommendations to the President.
p102: (8) A judge who is aggrieved by a decision of the tribunal under
p102: this Article may appeal against the decision to the Supreme Court,
p102: within ten days after the tribunal makes its recommendations.
p102: (9) The President shall act in accordance with the
p102: recommendations made by the tribunal on the later of—
p102: (a) the expiry of the time allowed for an appeal under clause (8),
p102: if no such appeal is taken; or
p102: (b) the completion of all rights of appeal in any proceedings
p102: allowed for under clause (8), if such an appeal is taken and
p102: the final order in the matter affirms the tribunal’s
p102: recommendations.
p102: (10) Parliament shall enact legislation providing for the procedure
p102: of a tribunal appointed under this Article.
p103: PART 3—SUBORDINATE COURTS
p103: Subordinate courts.
p103: 169. (1) The subordinate courts are—
p103: (a) the Magistrates courts;
p103: (b) the Kadhis’ courts;
p103: (c) the Courts Martial; and
p103: (d) any other court or local tribunal as may be established by an
p103: Act of Parliament, other than the courts established as
p103: required by Article 162 (2).
p103: (2) Parliament shall enact legislation conferring jurisdiction,
p103: functions and powers on the courts established under clause (1).
p103: Kadhis’ Courts.
p103: 170. (1) There shall be a Chief Kadhi and such number, being not
p103: fewer than three, of other Kadhis as may be prescribed under an Act of
p103: Parliament.
p103: (2) A person shall not be qualified to be appointed to hold or act
p103: in the office of Kadhi unless the person—
p103: (a) professes the Muslim religion; and
p103: (b) possesses such knowledge of the Muslim law applicable to
p103: any sects of Muslims as qualifies the person, in the opinion of
p103: the Judicial Service Commission, to hold a Kadhi’s court.
p103: (3) Parliament shall establish Kadhis’ courts, each of which shall
p103: have the jurisdiction and powers conferred on it by legislation, subject
p103: to clause (5).
p103: (4) The Chief Kadhi and the other Kadhis, or the Chief Kadhi and
p103: such of the other Kadhis (not being fewer than three in number) as
p103: may be prescribed under an Act of Parliament, shall each be
p103: empowered to hold a Kadhi’s court having jurisdiction within Kenya.
p103: (5) The jurisdiction of a Kadhis’ court shall be limited to the
p103: determination of questions of Muslim law relating to personal status,
p103: marriage, divorce or inheritance in proceedings in which all the parties
p103: profess the Muslim religion and submit to the jurisdiction of the Kadhi’s
p103: courts.
p104: PART 4—JUDICIAL SERVICE COMMISSION
p104: Establishment of the Judicial Service Commission.
p104: 171. (1) There is established the Judicial Service Commission.
p104: (2) The Commission shall consist of—
p104: (a) the Chief Justice, who shall be the chairperson of the
p104: Commission;
p104: (b) one Supreme Court judge elected by the judges of the
p104: Supreme Court;
p104: (c) one Court of Appeal judge elected by the judges of the Court
p104: of Appeal;
p104: (d) one High Court judge and one magistrate, one a woman and
p104: one a man, elected by the members of the association of
p104: judges and magistrates;
p104: (e) the Attorney-General;
p104: (f) two advocates, one a woman and one a man, each of whom
p104: has at least fifteen years’ experience, elected by the
p104: members of the statutory body responsible for the
p104: professional regulation of advocates;
p104: (g) one person nominated by the Public Service Commission;
p104: and
p104: (h) one woman and one man to represent the public, not being
p104: lawyers, appointed by the President with the approval of the
p104: National Assembly.
p104: (3) The Chief Registrar of the Judiciary shall be the Secretary to
p104: the Commission.
p104: (4) Members of the Commission, apart from the Chief Justice and
p104: the Attorney-General, shall hold office, provided that they remain
p104: qualified, for a term of five years and shall be eligible to be nominated
p104: for one further term of five years.
p104: Functions of the Judicial Service Commission.
p104: 172. (1) The Judicial Service Commission shall promote and
p104: facilitate the independence and accountability of the judiciary and the
p104: efficient, effective and transparent administration of justice and shall—
p104: (a) recommend to the President persons for appointment as
p104: judges;
p105: (b) review and make recommendations on the conditions of
p105: service of—
p105: (i) judges and judicial officers, other than their
p105: remuneration; and
p105: (ii) the staff of the Judiciary;
p105: (c) appoint, receive complaints against, investigate and remove
p105: from office or otherwise discipline registrars, magistrates,
p105: other judicial officers and other staff of the Judiciary, in the
p105: manner prescribed by an Act of Parliament;
p105: (d) prepare and implement programmes for the continuing
p105: education and training of judges and judicial officers; and
p105: (e) advise the national government on improving the efficiency of
p105: the administration of justice.
p105: (2) In the performance of its functions, the Commission shall be
p105: guided by the following—
p105: (a) competitiveness and transparent processes of appointment of
p105: judicial officers and other staff of the judiciary; and
p105: (b) the promotion of gender equality.
p105: Judiciary Fund.
p105: 173. (1) There is established a fund to be known as the Judiciary
p105: Fund which shall be administered by the Chief Registrar of the
p105: Judiciary.
p105: (2) The Fund shall be used for administrative expenses of the
p105: Judiciary and such other purposes as may be necessary for the
p105: discharge of the functions of the Judiciary.
p105: (3) Each financial year, the Chief Registrar shall prepare
p105: estimates of expenditure for the following year, and submit them to the
p105: National Assembly for approval.
p105: (4) On approval of the estimates by the National Assembly, the
p105: expenditure of the Judiciary shall be a charge on the Consolidated
p105: Fund and the funds shall be paid directly into the Judiciary Fund.
p105: (5) Parliament shall enact legislation to provide for the regulation
p105: of the Fund.
p106: CHAPTER ELEVEN—DEVOLVED GOVERNMENT
p106: PART 1—OBJECTS AND PRINCIPLES OF DEVOLVED GOVERNMENT
p106: Objects of devolution.
p106: 174. The objects of the devolution of government are—
p106: (a) to promote democratic and accountable exercise of power;
p106: (b) to foster national unity by recognising diversity;
p106: (c) to give powers of self-governance to the people and enhance
p106: the participation of the people in the exercise of the powers of
p106: the State and in making decisions affecting them;
p106: (d) to recognise the right of communities to manage their own
p106: affairs and to further their development;
p106: (e) to protect and promote the interests and rights of minorities
p106: and marginalised communities;
p106: (f) to promote social and economic development and the
p106: provision of proximate, easily accessible services throughout
p106: Kenya;
p106: (g) to ensure equitable sharing of national and local resources
p106: throughout Kenya;
p106: (h) to facilitate the decentralisation of State organs, their
p106: functions and services, from the capital of Kenya; and
p106: (i) to enhance checks and balances and the separation of
p106: powers.
p106: Principles of devolved government.
p106: 175. County governments established under this Constitution
p106: shall reflect the following principles—
p106: (a) county governments shall be based on democratic principles
p106: and the separation of powers;
p106: (b) county governments shall have reliable sources of revenue to
p106: enable them to govern and deliver services effectively; and
p106: (c) no more than two-thirds of the members of representative
p106: bodies in each county government shall be of the same
p106: gender.
p107: PART 2—COUNTY GOVERNMENTS
p107: County governments.
p107: 176. (1) There shall be a county government for each county,
p107: consisting of a county assembly and a county executive.
p107: (2) Every county government shall decentralise its functions and
p107: the provision of its services to the extent that it is efficient and
p107: practicable to do so.
p107: Membership of county assembly.
p107: 177. (1) A county assembly consists of—
p107: (a) members elected by the registered voters of the wards, each
p107: ward constituting a single member constituency, on the same
p107: day as a general election of Members of Parliament, being
p107: the second Tuesday in August, in every fifth year;
p107: (b) the number of special seat members necessary to ensure that
p107: no more than two-thirds of the membership of the assembly
p107: are of the same gender;
p107: (c) the number of members of marginalised groups, including
p107: persons with disabilities and the youth, prescribed by an Act
p107: of Parliament; and
p107: (d) the Speaker, who is an ex officio member.
p107: (2) The members contemplated in clause (1) (b) and (c) shall, in
p107: each case, be nominated by political parties in proportion to the seats
p107: received in that election in that county by each political party under
p107: paragraph (a) in accordance with Article 90.
p107: (3) The filling of special seats under clause (1) (b) shall be
p107: determined after declaration of elected members from each ward.
p107: (4) A county assembly is elected for a term of five years.
p107: Speaker of a county assembly.
p107: 178. (1) Each county assembly shall have a speaker elected by
p107: the county assembly from among persons who are not members of the
p107: assembly.
p107: (2) A sitting of the county assembly shall be presided over by—
p107: (a) the speaker of the assembly; or
p107: (b) in the absence of the speaker, another member of the
p107: assembly elected by the assembly.
p108: (3) Parliament shall enact legislation providing for the election
p108: and removal from office of speakers of the county assemblies.
p108: County executive committees.
p108: 179. (1) The executive authority of the county is vested in, and
p108: exercised by, a county executive committee.
p108: (2) The county executive committee consists of—
p108: (a) the county governor and the deputy county governor; and
p108: (b) members appointed by the county governor, with the approval
p108: of the assembly, from among persons who are not members
p108: of the assembly.
p108: (3) The number of members appointed under clause (2) (b) shall
p108: not exceed—
p108: (a) one-third of the number of members of the county assembly, if
p108: the assembly has less than thirty members; or
p108: (b) ten, if the assembly has thirty or more members.
p108: (4) The county governor and the deputy county governor are the
p108: chief executive and deputy chief executive of the county, respectively.
p108: (5) When the county governor is absent, the deputy county
p108: governor shall act as the county governor.
p108: (6) Members of a county executive committee are accountable to
p108: the county governor for the performance of their functions and exercise
p108: of their powers.
p108: (7) If a vacancy arises in the office of the county governor, the
p108: members of the county executive committee appointed under clause
p108: (2) (b) cease to hold office.
p108: Election of county governor and deputy county governor.
p108: 180. (1) The county governor shall be directly elected by the
p108: voters registered in the county, on the same day as a general election
p108: of Members of Parliament, being the second Tuesday in August, in
p108: every fifth year.
p108: (2) To be eligible for election as county governor, a person must
p108: be eligible for election as a member of the county assembly.
p108: (3) If only one candidate for county governor is nominated, that
p108: candidate shall be declared elected.
p109: (4) If two or more candidates are nominated, an election shall be
p109: held in the county and the candidate who receives the greatest number
p109: of votes shall be declared elected.
p109: (5) Each candidate for election as county governor shall
p109: nominate a person who is qualified for nomination for election as
p109: county governor as a candidate for deputy governor.
p109: (6) The Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission shall
p109: not conduct a separate election for the deputy governor but shall
p109: declare the candidate nominated by the person who is elected county
p109: governor to have been elected as the deputy governor.
p109: (7) A person shall not hold office—
p109: (a) as a county governor for more than two terms; or
p109: (b) as a deputy county governor for more than two terms.
p109: (8) For the purposes of clause (7), a person who has assumed
p109: the office of county governor shall be deemed to have served a full
p109: term, subject only to Article 182 (3) (b).
p109: Removal of a county governor.
p109: 181. (1) A county governor may be removed from office on any of
p109: the following grounds—
p109: (a) gross violation of this Constitution or any other law;
p109: (b) where there are serious reasons for believing that the county
p109: governor has committed a crime under national or
p109: international law;
p109: (c) abuse of office or gross misconduct; or
p109: (d) physical or mental incapacity to perform the functions of office
p109: of county governor.
p109: (2) Parliament shall enact legislation providing for the procedure
p109: of removal of a county governor on any of the grounds specified in
p109: clause (1).
p109: Vacancy in the office of county governor.
p109: 182. (1) The office of the county governor shall become vacant if
p109: the holder of the office—
p109: (a) dies;
p110: (b) resigns, in writing, addressed to the speaker of the county
p110: assembly;
p110: (c) ceases to be eligible to be elected county governor under
p110: Article 180 (2);
p110: (d) is convicted of an offence punishable by imprisonment for at
p110: least twelve months; or
p110: (e) is removed from office under this Constitution.
p110: (2) If a vacancy occurs in the office of county governor, the
p110: deputy county governor shall assume office as county governor for the
p110: remainder of the term of the county governor.
p110: (3) If a person assumes office as county governor under clause
p110: (2), the person shall be deemed for the purposes of Article 180 (7)—
p110: (a) to have served a full term as county governor if, at the date on
p110: which the person assumed office, more than two and a half
p110: years remain before the date of the next regularly scheduled
p110: election under Article 180 (1); or
p110: (b) not to have served a term of office as county governor, in any
p110: other case.
p110: (4) If a vacancy occurs in the office of county governor and that of
p110: deputy county governor, or if the deputy county governor is unable to
p110: act, the speaker of the county assembly shall act as county governor.
p110: (5) If a vacancy occurs in the circumstances contemplated by
p110: clause (4), an election to the office of county governor shall be held
p110: within sixty days after the speaker assumes the office of county
p110: governor.
p110: (6) A person who assumes the office of county governor under
p110: this Article shall, unless otherwise removed from office under this
p110: Constitution, hold office until the newly elected county governor
p110: assumes office following the next election held under Article 180 (1).
p110: Functions of county executive committees
p110: 183. (1) A county executive committee shall—
p110: (a) implement county legislation;
p110: (b) implement, within the county, national legislation to the extent
p110: that the legislation so requires;
p111: (c) manage and coordinate the functions of the county
p111: administration and its departments; and
p111: (d) perform any other functions conferred on it by this
p111: Constitution or national legislation.
p111: (2) A county executive committee may prepare proposed
p111: legislation for consideration by the county assembly.
p111: (3) The county executive committee shall provide the county
p111: assembly with full and regular reports on matters relating to the county.
p111: Urban areas and cities.
p111: 184. (1) National legislation shall provide for the governance
p111: and management of urban areas and cities and shall, in particular—
p111: (a) establish criteria for classifying areas as urban areas and
p111: cities,
p111: (b) establish the principles of governance and management of
p111: urban areas and cities; and
p111: (c) provide for participation by residents in the governance of
p111: urban areas and cities.
p111: (2) National legislation contemplated in clause (1) may include
p111: mechanisms for identifying different categories of urban areas and
p111: cities, and for their governance.
p111: Legislative authority of county assemblies.
p111: 185. (1) The legislative authority of a county is vested in, and
p111: exercised by, its county assembly.
p111: (2) A county assembly may make any laws that are necessary
p111: for, or incidental to, the effective performance of the functions and
p111: exercise of the powers of the county government under the Fourth
p111: Schedule.
p111: (3) A county assembly, while respecting the principle of the
p111: separation of powers, may exercise oversight over the county
p111: executive committee and any other county executive organs.
p111: (4) A county assembly may receive and approve plans and
p111: policies for—
p111: (a) the management and exploitation of the county’s resources;
p111: and
p112: (b) the development and management of its infrastructure and
p112: institutions.

p112: Respective functions and powers of national and county

p112: 186. (1) Except as otherwise provided by this Constitution, the
p112: functions and powers of the national government and the county
p112: governments, respectively, are as set out in the Fourth Schedule.
p112: (2) A function or power that is conferred on more than one level
p112: of government is a function or power within the concurrent jurisdiction
p112: of each of those levels of government.
p112: (3) A function or power not assigned by this Constitution or
p112: national legislation to a county is a function or power of the national
p112: government.
p112: (4) For greater certainty, Parliament may legislate for the
p112: Republic on any matter.
p112: Transfer of functions and powers between levels of government.
p112: 187. (1) A function or power of government at one level may be
p112: transferred to a government at the other level by agreement between
p112: the governments if—
p112: (a) the function or power would be more effectively performed or
p112: exercised by the receiving government; and
p112: (b) the transfer of the function or power is not prohibited by the
p112: legislation under which it is to be performed or exercised.
p112: (2) If a function or power is transferred from a government at one
p112: level to a government at the other level—
p112: (a) arrangements shall be put in place to ensure that the
p112: resources necessary for the performance of the function or
p112: exercise of the power are transferred; and
p112: (b) constitutional responsibility for the performance of the function
p112: or exercise of the power shall remain with the government to
p112: which it is assigned by the Fourth Schedule.
p113: PART 4—THE BOUNDARIES OF COUNTIES
p113: Boundaries of counties.
p113: 188. (1) The boundaries of a county may be altered only by a
p113: resolution—
p113: (a) recommended by an independent commission set up for that
p113: purpose by Parliament; and
p113: (b) passed by—
p113: (i) the National Assembly, with the support of at least
p113: two-thirds of all of the members of the Assembly; and
p113: (ii) the Senate, with the support of at least two-thirds of all of
p113: the county delegations.
p113: (2) The boundaries of a county may be altered to take into
p113: account—
p113: (a) population density and demographic trends;
p113: (b) physical and human infrastructure;
p113: (c) historical and cultural ties;
p113: (d) the cost of administration;
p113: (e) the views of the communities affected;
p113: (f) the objects of devolution of government; and
p113: (g) geographical features.
p113: PART 5—RELATIONSHIPS BETWEEN GOVERNMENTS
p113: Cooperation between national and county governments.
p113: 189. (1) Government at either level shall—
p113: (a) perform its functions, and exercise its powers, in a manner
p113: that respects the functional and institutional integrity of
p113: government at the other level, and respects the constitutional
p113: status and institutions of government at the other level and, in
p113: the case of county government, within the county level;
p113: (b) assist, support and consult and, as appropriate, implement
p113: the legislation of the other level of government; and
p113: (c) liaise with government at the other level for the purpose of
p114: exchanging information, coordinating policies and
p114: administration and enhancing capacity.
p114: (2) Government at each level, and different governments at the
p114: county level, shall co-operate in the performance of functions and
p114: exercise of powers and, for that purpose, may set up joint committees
p114: and joint authorities.
p114: (3) In any dispute between governments, the governments shall
p114: make every reasonable effort to settle the dispute, including by means
p114: of procedures provided under national legislation.
p114: (4) National legislation shall provide procedures for settling
p114: inter-governmental disputes by alternative dispute resolution
p114: mechanisms, including negotiation, mediation and arbitration.
p114: Support for county governments.
p114: 190. (1) Parliament shall by legislation ensure that county
p114: governments have adequate support to enable them to perform their
p114: functions.
p114: (2) County governments shall operate financial management
p114: systems that comply with any requirements prescribed by national
p114: legislation.
p114: (3) Parliament shall, by legislation, provide for intervention by the
p114: national government if a county government—
p114: (a) is unable to perform its functions; or
p114: (b) does not operate a financial management system that
p114: complies with the requirements prescribed by national
p114: legislation.
p114: (4) Legislation under clause (3) may, in particular, authorise the
p114: national government—
p114: (a) to take appropriate steps to ensure that the county
p114: government’s functions are performed and that it operates a
p114: financial management system that complies with the
p114: prescribed requirements; and
p114: (b) if necessary, to assume responsibility for the relevant
p114: functions.
p114: (5) The legislation under clause (3) shall—
p115: (a) require notice to be given to a county government of any
p115: measures that the national government intends to take;
p115: (b) permit the national government to take only measures that are
p115: necessary;
p115: (c) require the national government, when it intervenes, to take
p115: measures that will assist the county government to resume
p115: full responsibility for its functions; and
p115: (d) provide for a process by which the Senate may bring the
p115: intervention by the national government to an end.
p115: Conflict of laws.
p115: 191. (1) This Article applies to conflicts between national and
p115: county legislation in respect of matters falling within the concurrent
p115: jurisdiction of both levels of government.
p115: (2) National legislation prevails over county legislation if—
p115: (a) the national legislation applies uniformly throughout Kenya and
p115: any of the conditions specified in clause (3) is satisfied; or
p115: (b) the national legislation is aimed at preventing unreasonable
p115: action by a county that—
p115: (i) is prejudicial to the economic, health or security
p115: interests of Kenya or another county; or
p115: (ii) impedes the implementation of national economic policy.
p115: (3) The following are the conditions referred to in clause (2) (a)—
p115: (a) the national legislation provides for a matter that cannot be
p115: regulated effectively by legislation enacted by the individual
p115: counties;
p115: (b) the national legislation provides for a matter that, to be dealt
p115: with effectively, requires uniformity across the nation, and the
p115: national legislation provides that uniformity by establishing—
p115: (i) norms and standards; or
p115: (ii) national policies; or
p115: (c) the national legislation is necessary for—
p115: (i) the maintenance of national security;
p115: (ii) the maintenance of economic unity;
p116: (iii) the protection of the common market in respect of the
p116: mobility of goods, services, capital and labour;
p116: (iv) the promotion of economic activities across county
p116: boundaries;
p116: (v) the promotion of equal opportunity or equal access to
p116: government services; or
p116: (vi) the protection of the environment.
p116: (4) County legislation prevails over national legislation if neither
p116: of the circumstances contemplated in clause (2) apply.
p116: (5) In considering an apparent conflict between legislation of
p116: different levels of government, a court shall prefer a reasonable
p116: interpretation of the legislation that avoids a conflict to an alternative
p116: interpretation that results in conflict.
p116: (6) A decision by a court that a provision of legislation of one
p116: level of government prevails over a provision of legislation of another
p116: level of government does not invalidate the other provision, but the
p116: other provision is inoperative to the extent of the inconsistency.
p116: PART 6—SUSPENSION OF COUNTY GOVERNMENTS
p116: Suspension of a county government.
p116: 192. (1) The President may suspend a county government—
p116: (a) in an emergency arising out of internal conflict or war; or
p116: (b) in any other exceptional circumstances.
p116: (2) A county government shall not be suspended under clause (1)
p116: (b) unless an independent commission of inquiry has investigated
p116: allegations against the county government, the President is satisfied
p116: that the allegations are justified and the Senate has authorised the
p116: suspension.
p116: (3) During a suspension under this Article, arrangements shall be
p116: made for the performance of the functions of a county government in
p116: accordance with an Act of Parliament.
p116: (4) The Senate may at any time terminate the suspension.
p116: (5) A suspension under this Article shall not extend beyond a
p116: period of ninety days.
p117: (6) On the expiry of the period provided for under clause (5),
p117: elections for the relevant county government shall be held.
p117: PART 7—GENERAL
p117: Qualifications for election as member of county assembly.
p117: 193. (1) Unless disqualified under clause (2), a person is eligible
p117: for election as a member of a county assembly if the person—
p117: (a) is registered as a voter;
p117: (b) satisfies any educational, moral and ethical requirements
p117: prescribed by this Constitution or an Act of Parliament; and
p117: (c) is either—
p117: (i) nominated by a political party; or
p117: (ii) an independent candidate supported by at least five
p117: hundred registered voters in the ward concerned.
p117: (2) A person is disqualified from being elected a member of a
p117: county assembly if the person—
p117: (a) is a State officer or other public officer, other than a member
p117: of the county assembly;
p117: (b) has, at any time within the five years immediately before the
p117: date of election, held office as a member of the Independent
p117: Electoral and Boundaries Commission;
p117: (c) has not been a citizen of Kenya for at least the ten years
p117: immediately preceding the date of election;
p117: (d) is of unsound mind;
p117: (e) is an undischarged bankrupt;
p117: (f) is serving a sentence of imprisonment of at least six months;
p117: or
p117: (g) has been found, in accordance with any law, to have misused
p117: or abused a State office or public office or to have
p117: contravened Chapter Six.
p117: (3) A person is not disqualified under clause (2) unless all
p117: possibility of appeal or review of the relevant sentence or decision has
p117: been exhausted.
p118: Vacation of office of member of county assembly.
p118: 194. (1) The office of a member of a county assembly becomes
p118: vacant—
p118: (a) if the member dies;
p118: (b) if the member is absent from eight sittings of the assembly
p118: without permission, in writing, of the speaker of the assembly,
p118: and is unable to offer satisfactory explanation for the
p118: absence;
p118: (c) if the member is removed from office under this Constitution
p118: or legislation enacted under Article 80;
p118: (d) if the member resigns in writing addressed to the speaker of
p118: the assembly;
p118: (e) if, having been elected to the assembly—
p118: (i) as a member of a political party, the member resigns
p118: from the party, or is deemed to have resigned from the
p118: party as determined in accordance with the legislation
p118: contemplated in clause (2); or
p118: (ii) as an independent candidate, the member joins a political
p118: party;
p118: (f) at the end of the term of the assembly; or
p118: (g) if the member becomes disqualified for election on grounds
p118: specified in Article 193 (2).
p118: (2) Parliament shall enact legislation providing for the
p118: circumstances under which a member of a political party shall be
p118: deemed, for the purposes of clause (1) (e), to have resigned from the
p118: party.
p118: County assembly power to summon witnesses.
p118: 195. (1) A county assembly or any of its committees has power to
p118: summon any person to appear before it for the purpose of giving
p118: evidence or providing information.
p118: (2) For the purposes of clause (1), an assembly has the same
p118: powers as the High Court to—
p118: (a) enforce the attendance of witnesses and examining them on
p118: oath, affirmation or otherwise;
p118: (b) compel the production of documents; and
p119: (c) issue a commission or request to examine witnesses abroad.
p119: Public participation and county assembly powers, privileges and
p119: immunities.
p119: 196. (1) A county assembly shall—
p119: (a) conduct its business in an open manner, and hold its sittings
p119: and those of its committees, in public; and
p119: (b) facilitate public participation and involvement in the legislative
p119: and other business of the assembly and its committees.
p119: (2) A county assembly may not exclude the public, or any media,
p119: from any sitting unless in exceptional circumstances the speaker has
p119: determined that there are justifiable reasons for doing so.
p119: (3) Parliament shall enact legislation providing for the powers,
p119: privileges and immunities of county assemblies, their committees and
p119: members.
p119: County assembly gender balance and diversity.
p119: 197. (1) Not more than two-thirds of the members of any county
p119: assembly or county executive committee shall be of the same gender.
p119: (2) Parliament shall enact legislation to—
p119: (a) ensure that the community and cultural diversity of a county is
p119: reflected in its county assembly and county executive
p119: committee; and
p119: (b) prescribe mechanisms to protect minorities within counties.
p119: County government during transition.
p119: 198. While an election is being held to constitute a county
p119: assembly under this Chapter, the executive committee of the county,
p119: as last constituted remains competent to perform administrative
p119: functions until a new executive committee is constituted after the
p119: election.
p119: Publication of county legislation.
p119: 199. (1) County legislation does not take effect unless published
p119: in the Gazette.
p119: (2) National and county legislation may prescribe additional
p119: requirements in respect of the publication of county legislation.
p120: Legislation on Chapter.
p120: 200. (1) Parliament shall enact legislation providing for all matters
p120: necessary or convenient to give effect to this Chapter.
p120: (2) In particular, provision may be made with respect to—
p120: (a) the governance of the capital city, other cities and urban
p120: areas;
p120: (b) the transfer of functions and powers by one level of
p120: government to another, including the transfer of legislative
p120: powers from the national government to county governments;
p120: (c) the manner of election or appointment of persons to, and their
p120: removal from, offices in county governments, including the
p120: qualifications of voters and candidates;
p120: (d) the procedure of assemblies and executive committees
p120: including the chairing and frequency of meetings, quorums
p120: and voting; and
p120: (e) the suspension of assemblies and executive committees.
p120: CHAPTER TWELVE—PUBLIC FINANCE
p120: PART I—PRINCIPLES AND FRAMEWORK OF PUBLIC FINANCE
p120: Principles of public finance.
p120: 201. The following principles shall guide all aspects of public
p120: finance in the Republic—
p120: (a) there shall be openness and accountability, including public
p120: participation in financial matters;
p120: (b) the public finance system shall promote an equitable society,
p120: and in particular—
p120: (i) the burden of taxation shall be shared fairly;
p120: (ii) revenue raised nationally shall be shared equitably
p120: among national and county governments; and
p120: (iii) expenditure shall promote the equitable development of
p120: the country, including by making special provision for
p120: marginalised groups and areas;
p120: (c) the burdens and benefits of the use of resources and public
p120: borrowing shall be shared equitably between present and
p120: future generations;
p121: (d) public money shall be used in a prudent and responsible way;
p121: and
p121: (e) financial management shall be responsible, and fiscal
p121: reporting shall be clear.
p121: Equitable sharing of national revenue.
p121: 202. (1) Revenue raised nationally shall be shared equitably
p121: among the national and county governments.
p121: (2) County governments may be given additional allocations from
p121: the national government’s share of the revenue, either conditionally or
p121: unconditionally.
p121: Equitable share and other financial laws.
p121: 203. (1) The following criteria shall be taken into account in
p121: determining the equitable shares provided for under Article 202 and in
p121: all national legislation concerning county government enacted in terms
p121: of this Chapter—
p121: (a) the national interest;
p121: (b) any provision that must be made in respect of the public debt
p121: and other national obligations;
p121: (c) the needs of the national government, determined by
p121: objective criteria;
p121: (d) the need to ensure that county governments are able to
p121: perform the functions allocated to them;
p121: (e) the fiscal capacity and efficiency of county governments;
p121: (f) developmental and other needs of counties;
p121: (g) economic disparities within and among counties and the need
p121: to remedy them;
p121: (h) the need for affirmative action in respect of disadvantaged
p121: areas and groups;
p121: (i) the need for economic optimisation of each county and to
p121: provide incentives for each county to optimise its capacity to
p121: raise revenue;
p121: (j) the desirability of stable and predictable allocations of
p121: revenue; and
p122: (k) the need for flexibility in responding to emergencies and other
p122: temporary needs, based on similar objective criteria.
p122: (2) For every financial year, the equitable share of the revenue
p122: raised nationally that is allocated to county governments shall be not
p122: less than fifteen per cent of all revenue collected by the national
p122: government.
p122: (3) The amount referred to in clause (2) shall be calculated on the
p122: basis of the most recent audited accounts of revenue received, as
p122: approved by the National Assembly.
p122: Equalisation Fund.
p122: 204. (1) There is established an Equalisation Fund into which
p122: shall be paid one half per cent of all the revenue collected by the
p122: national government each year calculated on the basis of the most
p122: recent audited accounts of revenue received, as approved by the
p122: National Assembly.
p122: (2) The national government shall use the Equalisation Fund only
p122: to provide basic services including water, roads, health facilities and
p122: electricity to marginalised areas to the extent necessary to bring the
p122: quality of those services in those areas to the level generally enjoyed
p122: by the rest of the nation, so far as possible.
p122: (3) The national government may use the Equalisation Fund—
p122: (a) only to the extent that the expenditure of those funds has
p122: been approved in an Appropriation Bill enacted by
p122: Parliament; and
p122: (b) either directly, or indirectly through conditional grants to
p122: counties in which marginalised communities exist.
p122: (4) The Commission on Revenue Allocation shall be consulted
p122: and its recommendations considered before Parliament passes any
p122: Bill appropriating money out of the Equalisation Fund.
p122: (5) Any unexpended money in the Equalisation Fund at the end
p122: of a particular financial year shall remain in that Fund for use in
p122: accordance with clauses (2) and (3) during any subsequent financial
p122: year.
p122: (6) This Article lapses twenty years after the effective date,
p122: subject to clause (7).
p122: (7) Parliament may enact legislation suspending the effect of
p122: clause (6) for a further fixed period of years, subject to clause (8).
p123: (8) Legislation under clause (7) shall be supported by more than
p123: half of all the members of the National Assembly, and more than half
p123: of all the county delegations in the Senate.
p123: (9) Money shall not be withdrawn from the Equalisation Fund
p123: unless the Controller of Budget has approved the withdrawal.
p123: Consultation on financial legislation affecting counties.
p123: 205. (1) When a Bill that includes provisions dealing with the
p123: sharing of revenue, or any financial matter concerning county
p123: governments is published, the Commission on Revenue Allocation
p123: shall consider those provisions and may make recommendations to
p123: the National Assembly and the Senate.
p123: (2) Any recommendations made by the Commission shall be
p123: tabled in Parliament, and each House shall consider the
p123: recommendations before voting on the Bill.
p123: PART 2—OTHER PUBLIC FUNDS
p123: Consolidated Fund and other public funds.
p123: 206. (1) There is established the Consolidated Fund into which
p123: shall be paid all money raised or received by or on behalf of the
p123: national government, except money that—
p123: (a) is reasonably excluded from the Fund by an Act of Parliament
p123: and payable into another public fund established for a specific
p123: purpose; or
p123: (b) may, under an Act of Parliament, be retained by the State
p123: organ that received it for the purpose of defraying the
p123: expenses of the State organ.
p123: (2) Money may be withdrawn from the Consolidated Fund only—
p123: (a) in accordance with an appropriation by an Act of Parliament;
p123: (b) in accordance with Article 222 or 223; or
p123: (c) as a charge against the Fund as authorised by this
p123: Constitution or an Act of Parliament.
p123: (3) Money shall not be withdrawn from any national public fund
p123: other than the Consolidated Fund, unless the withdrawal of the money
p123: has been authorised by an Act of Parliament.
p123: (4) Money shall not be withdrawn from the Consolidated Fund
p123: unless the Controller of Budget has approved the withdrawal.
p124: Revenue Funds for county governments.
p124: 207. (1) There shall be established a Revenue Fund for each
p124: county government, into which shall be paid all money raised or
p124: received by or on behalf of the county government, except money
p124: reasonably excluded by an Act of Parliament.
p124: (2) Money may be withdrawn from the Revenue Fund of a county
p124: government only—
p124: (a) as a charge against the Revenue Fund that is provided for by
p124: an Act of Parliament or by legislation of the county; or
p124: (b) as authorised by an appropriation by legislation of the county.
p124: (3) Money shall not be withdrawn from a Revenue Fund unless
p124: the Controller of Budget has approved the withdrawal.
p124: (4) An Act of Parliament may—
p124: (a) make further provision for the withdrawal of funds from a
p124: county Revenue Fund; and
p124: (b) provide for the establishment of other funds by counties and
p124: the management of those funds.
p124: Contingencies Fund.
p124: 208. (1) There is established a Contingencies Fund, the
p124: operation of which shall be in accordance with an Act of Parliament.
p124: (2) An Act of Parliament shall provide for advances from the
p124: Contingencies Fund if the Cabinet Secretary responsible for finance is
p124: satisfied that there is an urgent and unforeseen need for expenditure
p124: for which there is no other authority.
p124: PART 3—REVENUE-RAISING POWERS AND THE PUBLIC DEBT
p124: Power to impose taxes and charges.
p124: 209. (1) Only the national government may impose—
p124: (a) income tax;
p124: (b) value-added tax;
p124: (c) customs duties and other duties on import and export goods;
p124: and
p124: (d) excise tax.
p125: (2) An Act of Parliament may authorise the national government
p125: to impose any other tax or duty, except a tax specified in clause (3) (a)
p125: or (b).
p125: (3) A county may impose—
p125: (a) property rates;
p125: (b) entertainment taxes; and
p125: (c) any other tax that it is authorised to impose by an Act of
p125: Parliament.
p125: (4) The national and county governments may impose charges
p125: for the services they provide.
p125: (5) The taxation and other revenue-raising powers of a county
p125: shall not be exercised in a way that prejudices national economic
p125: policies, economic activities across county boundaries or the national
p125: mobility of goods, services, capital or labour.
p125: Imposition of tax.
p125: 210. (1) No tax or licensing fee may be imposed, waived or
p125: varied except as provided by legislation.
p125: (2) If legislation permits the waiver of any tax or licensing fee—
p125: (a) a public record of each waiver shall be maintained together
p125: with the reason for the waiver; and
p125: (b) each waiver, and the reason for it, shall be reported to the
p125: Auditor-General.
p125: (3) No law may exclude or authorise the exclusion of a State
p125: officer from payment of tax by reason of—
p125: (a) the office held by that State officer; or
p125: (b) the nature of the work of the State officer.
p125: Borrowing by national government.
p125: 211. (1) Parliament may, by legislation—
p125: (a) prescribe the terms on which the national government may
p125: borrow; and
p125: (b) impose reporting requirements.
p126: (2) Within seven days after either House of Parliament so
p126: requests by resolution, the Cabinet Secretary responsible for finance
p126: shall present to the relevant committee, information concerning any
p126: particular loan or guarantee, including all information necessary to
p126: show—
p126: (a) the extent of the total indebtedness by way of principal and
p126: accumulated interest;
p126: (b) the use made or to be made of the proceeds of the loan;
p126: (c) the provision made for servicing or repayment of the loan; and
p126: (d) the progress made in the repayment of the loan.
p126: Borrowing by counties.
p126: 212. A county government may borrow only—
p126: (a) if the national government guarantees the loan; and
p126: (b) with the approval of the county government’s assembly.
p126: Loan guarantees by national government.
p126: 213. (1) An Act of Parliament shall prescribe terms and
p126: conditions under which the national government may guarantee loans.
p126: (2) Within two months after the end of each financial year, the
p126: national government shall publish a report on the guarantees that it
p126: gave during that year.
p126: Public debt.
p126: 214. (1) The public debt is a charge on the Consolidated Fund,
p126: but an Act of Parliament may provide for charging all or part of the
p126: public debt to other public funds.
p126: (2) For the purposes of this Article, “the public debt” means all
p126: financial obligations attendant to loans raised or guaranteed and
p126: securities issued or guaranteed by the national government.
p126: PART 4—REVENUE ALLOCATION
p126: Commission on Revenue Allocation.
p126: 215. (1) There is established the Commission on Revenue
p126: Allocation.
p126: (2) The Commission shall consist of the following persons
p126: appointed by the President—
p127: (a) a chairperson, who shall be nominated by the President and
p127: approved by the National Assembly;
p127: (b) two persons nominated by the political parties represented in
p127: the National Assembly according to their proportion of
p127: members in the Assembly;
p127: (c) five persons nominated by the political parties represented in
p127: the Senate according to their proportion of members in the
p127: Senate; and
p127: (d) the Principal Secretary in the Ministry responsible for finance.
p127: (3) The persons nominated under clause (2) shall not be
p127: members of Parliament.
p127: (4) To be qualified to be a member of the Commission under
p127: clause (2) (a), (b) or (c), a person shall have extensive professional
p127: experience in financial and economic matters.
p127: Functions of the Commission on Revenue Allocation.
p127: 216. (1) The principal function of the Commission on Revenue
p127: Allocation is to make recommendations concerning the basis for the
p127: equitable sharing of revenue raised by the national government—
p127: (a) between the national and county governments; and
p127: (b) among the county governments.
p127: (2) The Commission shall also make recommendations on other
p127: matters concerning the financing of, and financial management by,
p127: county governments, as required by this Constitution and national
p127: legislation.
p127: (3) In formulating recommendations, the Commission shall seek
p127: —
p127: (a) to promote and give effect to the criteria set out in Article 203
p127: (1);
p127: (b) when appropriate, to define and enhance the revenue sources
p127: of the national and county governments; and
p127: (c) to encourage fiscal responsibility.
p127: (4) The Commission shall determine, publish and regularly review
p127: a policy in which it sets out the criteria by which to identify the
p127: marginalised areas for purposes of Article 204 (2).
p128: (5) The Commission shall submit its recommendations to the
p128: Senate, the National Assembly, the national executive, county
p128: assemblies and county executives.
p128: Division of revenue.
p128: 217. (1) Once every five years, the Senate shall, by resolution,
p128: determine the basis for allocating among the counties the share of
p128: national revenue that is annually allocated to the county level of
p128: government.
p128: (2) In determining the basis of revenue sharing under clause (1),
p128: the Senate shall—
p128: (a) take the criteria in Article 203 (1) into account;
p128: (b) request and consider recommendations from the Commission
p128: on Revenue Allocation;
p128: (c) consult the county governors, the Cabinet Secretary
p128: responsible for finance and any organisation of county
p128: governments; and
p128: (d) invite the public, including professional bodies, to make
p128: submissions to it on the matter.
p128: (3) Within ten days after the Senate adopts a resolution under
p128: clause (1), the Speaker of the Senate shall refer the resolution to the
p128: Speaker of the National Assembly.
p128: (4) Within sixty days after the Senate’s resolution is referred
p128: under clause (3), the National Assembly may consider the resolution,
p128: and vote to approve it, with or without amendments, or to reject it.
p128: (5) If the National Assembly—
p128: (a) does not vote on the resolution within sixty days, the
p128: resolution shall be regarded as having been approved by the
p128: National Assembly without amendment; or
p128: (b) votes on the resolution, the resolution shall have been—
p128: (i) amended only if at least two-thirds of the members of the
p128: Assembly vote in support of an amendment;
p128: (ii) rejected only if at least two-thirds of the members of the
p128: Assembly vote against it, irrespective whether it has
p128: first been amended by the Assembly; or
p128: (iii) approved, in any other case.
p129: (6) If the National Assembly approves an amended version of the
p129: resolution, or rejects the resolution, the Senate, at its option, may
p129: either—
p129: (a) adopt a new resolution under clause (1), in which case the
p129: provisions of this clause and clause (4) and (5) apply afresh;

p129: two Houses of Parliament for mediation under Article 113,
p129: applied with the necessary modifications.
p129: (7) A resolution under this Article that is approved under clause
p129: (5) shall be binding until a subsequent resolution has been approved.
p129: (8) Despite clause (1), the Senate may, by resolution supported
p129: by at least two-thirds of its members, amend a resolution at any time
p129: after it has been approved.
p129: (9) Clauses (2) to (8), with the necessary modifications, apply to
p129: a resolution under clause (8).
p129: Annual Division and Allocation of Revenue Bills.
p129: 218. (1) At least two months before the end of each financial

p129: (b) a County Allocation of Revenue Bill, which shall divide among
p129: the counties the revenue allocated to the county level of
p129: government on the basis determined in accordance with the

p129: (2) Each Bill required by clause (1) shall be accompanied by a

p129: (a) an explanation of revenue allocation as proposed by the Bill;
p129: (b) an evaluation of the Bill in relation to the criteria set out in
p129: Article 203 (1); and
p129: (c) a summary of any significant deviation from the Commission
p129: on Revenue Allocation’s recommendations, with an
p129: explanation for each such deviation.
p130: Transfer of equitable share.
p130: 219. A county’s share of revenue raised by the national
p130: government shall be transferred to the county without undue delay and
p130: without deduction, except when the transfer has been stopped under
p130: Article 225.
p130: PART 5—BUDGETS AND SPENDING
p130: Form, content and timing of budgets.
p130: 220. (1) Budgets of the national and county governments shall
p130: contain—

p130: which they apply; and

p130: (2) National legislation shall prescribe—

p130: the county assemblies; and

p130: 221. (1) At least two months before the end of each financial
p130: year, the Cabinet Secretary responsible for finance shall submit to the
p130: National Assembly estimates of the revenue and expenditure of the
p130: national government for the next financial year to be tabled in the
p130: National Assembly.
p130: (2) The estimates referred to in clause (1) shall—
p130: (a) include estimates for expenditure from the Equalisation Fund;
p130: and
p130: (b) be in the form, and according to the procedure, prescribed by
p130: an Act of Parliament.
p130: (3) The National Assembly shall consider the estimates submitted
p131: under clause (1) together with the estimates submitted by the
p131: Parliamentary Service Commission and the Chief Registrar of the
p131: Judiciary under Articles 127 and 173 respectively.
p131: (4) Before the National Assembly considers the estimates of
p131: revenue and expenditure, a committee of the Assembly shall discuss
p131: and review the estimates and make recommendations to the
p131: Assembly.
p131: (5) In discussing and reviewing the estimates, the committee
p131: shall seek representations from the public and the recommendations
p131: shall be taken into account when the committee makes its
p131: recommendations to the National Assembly.
p131: (6) When the estimates of national government expenditure, and
p131: the estimates of expenditure for the Judiciary and Parliament have
p131: been approved by the National Assembly, they shall be included in an
p131: Appropriation Bill, which shall be introduced into the National
p131: Assembly to authorise the withdrawal from the Consolidated Fund of
p131: the money needed for the expenditure, and for the appropriation of that
p131: money for the purposes mentioned in the Bill.
p131: (7) The Appropriation Bill mentioned in clause (6) shall not
p131: include expenditures that are charged on the Consolidated Fund by
p131: this Constitution or an Act of Parliament.
p131: Expenditure before annual budget is passed
p131: 222. (1) If the Appropriation Act for a financial year has not been
p131: assented to, or is not likely to be assented to, by the beginning of that
p131: financial year, the National Assembly may authorise the withdrawal of
p131: money from the Consolidated Fund.
p131: (2) Money withdrawn under clause (1) shall—
p131: (a) be for the purpose of meeting expenditure necessary to carry
p131: on the services of the national government during that year
p131: until such time as the Appropriation Act is assented to;
p131: (b) not exceed in total one-half of the amount included in the
p131: estimates of expenditure for that year that have been tabled
p131: in the National Assembly; and
p131: (c) be included, under separate votes for the several services in
p131: respect of which they were withdrawn, in the Appropriation
p131: Act.
p131: Supplementary appropriation.
p131: 223. (1) Subject to clauses (2) to (4), the national government
p132: may spend money that has not been appropriated if—
p132: (a) the amount appropriated for any purpose under the
p132: Appropriation Act is insufficient or a need has arisen for
p132: expenditure for a purpose for which no amount has been
p132: appropriated by that Act; or
p132: (b) money has been withdrawn from the Contingencies Fund.
p132: (2) The approval of Parliament for any spending under this Article
p132: shall be sought within two months after the first withdrawal of the
p132: money, subject to clause (3).
p132: (3) If Parliament is not sitting during the time contemplated in
p132: clause (2), or is sitting but adjourns before the approval has been
p132: sought, the approval shall be sought within two weeks after it next sits.
p132: (4) When the National Assembly has approved spending under
p132: clause (2), an appropriation Bill shall be introduced for the
p132: appropriation of the money spent.
p132: (5) In any particular financial year, the national government may
p132: not spend under this Article more than ten per cent of the sum
p132: appropriated by Parliament for that financial year unless, in special
p132: circumstances, Parliament has approved a higher percentage.
p132: County appropriation Bills.
p132: 224. On the basis of the Division of Revenue Bill passed by
p132: Parliament under Article 218, each county government shall prepare
p132: and adopt its own annual budget and appropriation Bill in the form, and
p132: according to the procedure, prescribed in an Act of Parliament.
p132: PART 6—CONTROL OF PUBLIC MONEY
p132: Financial control.
p132: 225. (1) An Act of Parliament shall provide for the establishment,
p132: functions and responsibilities of the national Treasury.
p132: (2) Parliament shall enact legislation to ensure both expenditure
p132: control and transparency in all governments and establish
p132: mechanisms to ensure their implementation.
p132: (3) Legislation under clause (2) may authorise the Cabinet
p132: Secretary responsible for finance to stop the transfer of funds to a
p132: State organ or any other public entity—
p133: (a) only for a serious material breach or persistent material
p133: breaches of the measures established under that legislation;
p133: and
p133: (b) subject to the requirements of clauses (4) to (7).
p133: (4) A decision to stop the transfer of funds under clause (3) may
p133: not stop the transfer of more than fifty per cent of funds due to a
p133: county government.
p133: (5) A decision to stop the transfer of funds as contemplated in
p133: clause (3)—
p133: (a) shall not stop the transfer of funds for more than sixty days;
p133: and
p133: (b) may be enforced immediately, but will lapse retrospectively
p133: unless, within thirty days after the date of the decision,
p133: Parliament approves it by resolution passed by both Houses.
p133: (6) Parliament may renew a decision to stop the transfer of funds
p133: but for no more than sixty days at a time.
p133: (7) Parliament may not approve or renew a decision to stop the
p133: transfer of funds unless—
p133: (a) the Controller of Budget has presented a report on the matter
p133: to Parliament; and
p133: (b) the public entity has been given an opportunity to answer the
p133: allegations against it, and to state its case, before the
p133: relevant parliamentary committee.
p133: Accounts and audit of public entities.
p133: 226. (1) An Act of Parliament shall provide for—
p133: (a) the keeping of financial records and the auditing of accounts
p133: of all governments and other public entities, and prescribe
p133: other measures for securing efficient and transparent fiscal
p133: management; and
p133: (b) the designation of an accounting officer in every public entity
p133: at the national and county level of government.
p133: (2) The accounting officer of a national public entity is
p133: accountable to the National Assembly for its financial management,
p133: and the accounting officer of a county public entity is accountable to
p133: the county assembly for its financial management.
p134: (3) Subject to clause (4), the accounts of all governments and
p134: State organs shall be audited by the Auditor-General.
p134: (4) The accounts of the office of the Auditor-General shall be
p134: audited and reported on by a professionally qualified accountant
p134: appointed by the National Assembly.
p134: (5) If the holder of a public office, including a political office,
p134: directs or approves the use of public funds contrary to law or
p134: instructions, the person is liable for any loss arising from that use and
p134: shall make good the loss, whether the person remains the holder of
p134: the office or not.
p134: Procurement of public goods and services.
p134: 227. (1) When a State organ or any other public entity contracts
p134: for goods or services, it shall do so in accordance with a system that is
p134: fair, equitable, transparent, competitive and cost-effective.
p134: (2) An Act of Parliament shall prescribe a framework within which
p134: policies relating to procurement and asset disposal shall be
p134: implemented and may provide for all or any of the following—
p134: (a) categories of preference in the allocation of contracts;
p134: (b) the protection or advancement of persons, categories of
p134: persons or groups previously disadvantaged by unfair
p134: competition or discrimination;
p134: (c) sanctions against contractors that have not performed
p134: according to professionally regulated procedures, contractual
p134: agreements or legislation; and
p134: (d) sanctions against persons who have defaulted on their tax
p134: obligations, or have been guilty of corrupt practices or serious
p134: violations of fair employment laws and practices.

p138: CHAPTER THIRTEEN—THE PUBLIC SERVICE

p138: service in—
p138: (a) all State organs in both levels of government; and

p142: CHAPTER FOURTEEN—NATIONAL SECURITY
p142: PART 1—NATIONAL SECURITY ORGANS
p142: Principles of national security.
p142: 238. (1) National security is the protection against internal and
p142: external threats to Kenya’s territorial integrity and sovereignty, its
p142: people, their rights, freedoms, property, peace, stability and prosperity,
p142: and other national interests.
p142: (2) The national security of Kenya shall be promoted and
p142: guaranteed in accordance with the following principles—
p142: (a) national security is subject to the authority of this Constitution
p142: and Parliament;
p142: (b) national security shall be pursued in compliance with the law
p142: and with the utmost respect for the rule of law, democracy,
p142: human rights and fundamental freedoms;
p142: (c) in performing their functions and exercising their powers,
p142: national security organs shall respect the diverse culture of
p142: the communities within Kenya; and
p142: (d) recruitment by the national security organs shall reflect the
p142: diversity of the Kenyan people in equitable proportions.
p142: National security organs.
p142: 239. (1) The national security organs are—
p143: (a) the Kenya Defence Forces;
p143: (b) the National Intelligence Service; and
p143: (c) the National Police Service.
p143: (2) The primary object of the national security organs and
p143: security system is to promote and guarantee national security in
p143: accordance with the principles mentioned in Article 238 (2).
p143: (3) In performing their functions and exercising their powers, the
p143: national security organs and every member of the national security
p143: organs shall not—
p143: (a) act in a partisan manner;
p143: (b) further any interest of a political party or cause; or
p143: (c) prejudice a political interest or political cause that is legitimate
p143: under this Constitution.
p143: (4) A person shall not establish a military, paramilitary, or similar
p143: organisation that purports to promote and guarantee national security,
p143: except as provided for by this Constitution or an Act of Parliament.
p143: (5) The national security organs are subordinate to civilian
p143: authority.
p143: (6) Parliament shall enact legislation to provide for the functions,
p143: organisation and administration of the national security organs.
p143: Establishment of the National Security Council
p143: 240. (1) There is established a National Security Council.
p143: (2) The Council consists of—
p143: (a) the President;
p143: (b) the Deputy President;
p143: (c) the Cabinet Secretary responsible for defence;
p143: (d) the Cabinet Secretary responsible for foreign affairs;
p143: (e) the Cabinet Secretary responsible for internal security;
p143: (f) the Attorney-General;
p143: (g) the Chief of Kenya Defence Forces;
p144: (h) the Director-General of the National Intelligence Service; and
p144: (i) the Inspector-General of the National Police Service.
p144: (3) The Council shall exercise supervisory control over national
p144: security organs and perform any other functions prescribed by national
p144: legislation.
p144: (4) The President shall preside at meetings of the Council.
p144: (5) The Council shall appoint its secretary.
p144: (6) The Council shall—
p144: (a) integrate the domestic, foreign and military policies relating to
p144: national security in order to enable the national security
p144: organs to co-operate and function effectively; and
p144: (b) assess and appraise the objectives, commitments and risks to
p144: the Republic in respect of actual and potential national
p144: security capabilities.
p144: (7) The Council shall report annually to Parliament on the state of
p144: the security of Kenya.
p144: (8) The Council may, with the approval of Parliament—
p144: (a) deploy national forces outside Kenya for—
p144: (i) regional or international peace support operations; or
p144: (ii) other support operations; and
p144: (b) approve the deployment of foreign forces in Kenya.
p144: PART 2—THE KENYA DEFENCE FORCES
p144: Establishment of Defence Forces and Defence Council.
p144: 241. (1) There are established the Kenya Defence Forces.
p144: (2) The Defence Forces consist of—
p144: (a) the Kenya Army;
p144: (b) the Kenya Air Force; and
p144: (c) the Kenya Navy.
p144: (3) The Defence Forces—
p145: (a) are responsible for the defence and protection of the
p145: sovereignty and territorial integrity of the Republic;
p145: (b) shall assist and cooperate with other authorities in situations
p145: of emergency or disaster, and report to the National
p145: Assembly whenever deployed in such circumstances; and
p145: (c) may be deployed to restore peace in any part of Kenya
p145: affected by unrest or instability only with the approval of the
p145: National Assembly.
p145: (4) The composition of the command of the Defence Forces shall
p145: reflect the regional and ethnic diversity of the people of Kenya.
p145: (5) There is established a Defence Council.
p145: (6) The Council consist of—
p145: (a) the Cabinet Secretary responsible for defence, who is the
p145: chairperson;
p145: (b) the Chief of the Kenya Defence Forces;
p145: (c) the three commanders of the defence forces; and
p145: (d) the Principal Secretary in the Ministry responsible for defence.
p145: (7) The Council—
p145: (a) is responsible for the overall policy, control, and supervision of
p145: the Kenya Defence Forces; and
p145: (b) performs any other functions prescribed by national
p145: legislation.
p145: PART 3—THE NATIONAL INTELLIGENCE SERVICE
p145: Establishment of National Intelligence Service.
p145: 242. (1) There is established the National Intelligence Service.
p145: (2) The National Intelligence Service—
p145: (a) is responsible for security intelligence and counter intelligence
p145: to enhance national security in accordance with this
p145: Constitution; and
p145: (b) performs any other functions prescribed by national
p145: legislation.
p146: PART 4—THE NATIONAL POLICE SERVICE
p146: Establishment of the National Police Service.
p146: 243. (1) There is established the National Police Service.
p146: (2) The National Police Service consists of—
p146: (a) the Kenya Police Service; and
p146: (b) the Administration Police Service.
p146: (3) The National Police Service is a national service and shall
p146: function throughout Kenya.
p146: (4) Parliament shall enact legislation to give full effect to this
p146: Article.
p146: Objects and functions of the National Police Service.
p146: 244. The National Police Service shall—
p146: (a) strive for the highest standards of professionalism and
p146: discipline among its members;
p146: (b) prevent corruption and promote and practice transparency
p146: and accountability;
p146: (c) comply with constitutional standards of human rights and
p146: fundamental freedoms;
p146: (d) train staff to the highest possible standards of competence
p146: and integrity and to respect human rights and fundamental
p146: freedoms and dignity; and
p146: (e) foster and promote relationships with the broader society.
p146: Command of the National Police Service.
p146: 245. (1) There is established the office of the Inspector-General
p146: of the National Police Service.
p146: (2) The Inspector-General—
p146: (a) is appointed by the President with the approval of Parliament;
p146: and
p146: (b) shall exercise independent command over the National Police
p146: Service, and perform any other functions prescribed by
p146: national legislation.
p147: (3) The Kenya Police Service and the Administration Police
p147: Service shall each be headed by a Deputy Inspector-General
p147: appointed by the President in accordance with the recommendation of
p147: the National Police Service Commission.
p147: (4) The Cabinet secretary responsible for police services may
p147: lawfully give a direction to the Inspector-General with respect to any
p147: matter of policy for the National Police Service, but no person may give
p147: a direction to the Inspector-General with respect to—
p147: (a) the investigation of any particular offence or offences;
p147: (b) the enforcement of the law against any particular person or
p147: persons; or
p147: (c) the employment, assignment, promotion, suspension or
p147: dismissal of any member of the National Police Service.
p147: (5) Any direction given to the Inspector-General by the Cabinet
p147: secretary responsible for police services under clause (4), or any
p147: direction given to the Inspector-General by the Director of Public
p147: Prosecutions under Article 157(4), shall be in writing.
p147: (6) The Inspector-General shall be appointed for a single
p147: four-year term, and is not eligible for re-appointment.
p147: (7) The Inspector-General may be removed from office by the
p147: President only on the grounds of—
p147: (a) serious violation of this Constitution or any other law,
p147: including a contravention of Chapter Six;
p147: (b) gross misconduct whether in the performance of the office
p147: holder’s functions or otherwise;
p147: (c) physical or mental incapacity to perform the functions of
p147: office;
p147: (d) incompetence;
p147: (e) bankruptcy; or
p147: (f) any other just cause.
p147: (8) Parliament shall enact legislation to give full effect to this
p147: Article.
p147: National Police Service Commission.
p147: 246. (1) There is established the National Police Service
p148: Commission.
p148: (2) The Commission consists of—
p148: (a) the following persons, each appointed by the President—
p148: (i) a person who is qualified to be appointed as a High
p148: Court Judge;
p148: (ii) two retired senior police officers; and
p148: (iii) three persons of integrity who have served the public with
p148: distinction;
p148: (b) the Inspector-General of the National Police Service; and
p148: (c) both Deputy Inspectors-General of the National Police
p148: Service.
p148: (3) The Commission shall—
p148: (a) recruit and appoint persons to hold or act in offices in the
p148: service, confirm appointments, and determine promotions
p148: and transfers within the National Police Service;
p148: (b) observing due process, exercise disciplinary control over and
p148: remove persons holding or acting in offices within the
p148: Service; and
p148: (c) perform any other functions prescribed by national legislation.
p148: (4) The composition of the National Police Service shall reflect
p148: the regional and ethnic diversity of the people of Kenya.
p148: Other police services.
p148: 247. Parliament may enact legislation establishing other police
p148: services under the supervision of the National Police Service and the
p148: command of the Inspector-General of the Service.
p148: CHAPTER FIFTEEN—COMMISSIONS AND INDEPENDENT
p148: OFFICES
p148: Application of Chapter.
p148: 248. (1) This Chapter applies to the commissions specified in
p148: clause (2) and the independent offices specified in clause (3), except
p148: to the extent that this Constitution provides otherwise.
p148: (2) The commissions are—
p149: (a) the Kenya National Human Rights and Equality Commission;
p149: (b) the National Land Commission;
p149: (c) the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission;
p149: (d) the Parliamentary Service Commission;
p149: (e) the Judicial Service Commission;
p149: (f) the Commission on Revenue Allocation;
p149: (g) the Public Service Commission;
p149: (h) the Salaries and Remuneration Commission;
p149: (i) the Teachers Service Commission; and
p149: (j) the National Police Service Commission.
p149: (3) The independent offices are—
p149: (a) the Auditor-General; and
p149: (b) the Controller of Budget.
p149: Objects, authority and funding of commissions and independent
p149: offices.
p149: 249. (1) The objects of the commissions and the independent
p149: offices are to—
p149: (a) protect the sovereignty of the people;
p149: (b) secure the observance by all State organs of democratic
p149: values and principles; and
p149: (c) promote constitutionalism.
p149: (2) The commissions and the holders of independent offices—
p149: (a) are subject only to this Constitution and the law; and
p149: (b) are independent and not subject to direction or control by any
p149: person or authority.
p149: (3) Parliament shall allocate adequate funds to enable each
p149: commission and independent office to perform its functions and the
p149: budget of each commission and independent office shall be a separate
p149: vote.
p150: Composition, appointment and terms of office.
p150: 250. (1) Each commission shall consist of at least three, but not
p150: more than nine, members.
p150: (2) The chairperson and each member of a commission, and the
p150: holder of an independent office, shall be—
p150: (a) identified and recommended for appointment in a manner
p150: prescribed by national legislation;
p150: (b) approved by the National Assembly; and
p150: (c) appointed by the President.
p150: (3) To be appointed, a person shall have the specific
p150: qualifications required by this Constitution or national legislation.
p150: (4) Appointments to commissions and independent offices shall
p150: take into account the national values referred to in Article 10, and the
p150: principle that the composition of the commissions and offices, taken as
p150: a whole, shall reflect the regional and ethnic diversity of the people of
p150: Kenya.
p150: (5) A member of a commission may serve on a part-time basis.
p150: (6) A member of a commission, or the holder of an independent
p150: office—
p150: (a) unless ex officio, shall be appointed for a single term of six
p150: years and is not eligible for re-appointment; and
p150: (b) unless ex officio or part-time, shall not hold any other office or
p150: employment for profit, whether public or private.
p150: (7) The remuneration and benefits payable to or in respect of a
p150: commissioner or the holder of an independent office shall be a charge
p150: on the Consolidated Fund.
p150: (8) The remuneration and benefits payable to, or in respect of, a
p150: commissioner or the holder of an independent office shall not be varied
p150: to the disadvantage of that commissioner or holder of an independent
p150: office.
p150: (9) A member of a commission, or the holder of an independent
p150: office, is not liable for anything done in good faith in the performance of
p150: a function of office.
p151: (10) The members of a commission shall elect a vice-chairperson
p151: from among themselves—
p151: (a) at the first sitting of the commission; and
p151: (b) whenever it is necessary to fill a vacancy in the office of the
p151: vice-chairperson.
p151: (11) The chairperson and vice-chairperson of a commission shall
p151: not be of the same gender.
p151: (12) There shall be a Secretary to each commission who shall be
p151: —
p151: (a) appointed by the commission; and
p151: (b) the chief executive officer of the commission.
p151: Removal from office.
p151: 251. (1) A member of a commission (other than an ex officio
p151: member), or the holder of an independent office, may be removed from
p151: office only for—
p151: (a) serious violation of this Constitution or any other law,
p151: including a contravention of Chapter Six;
p151: (b) gross misconduct, whether in the performance of the
p151: member’s or office holder’s functions or otherwise;
p151: (c) physical or mental incapacity to perform the functions of
p151: office;
p151: (d) incompetence; or
p151: (e) bankruptcy.
p151: (2) A person desiring the removal of a member of a commission
p151: or of a holder of an independent office on any ground specified in
p151: clause (1) may present a petition to the National Assembly setting out
p151: the alleged facts constituting that ground.
p151: (3) The National Assembly shall consider the petition and, if it is
p151: satisfied that it discloses a ground under clause (1), shall send the
p151: petition to the President.
p151: (4) On receiving a petition under clause (3), the President—
p151: (a) may suspend the member or office holder pending the
p151: outcome of the complaint; and
p152: (b) shall appoint a tribunal in accordance with clause (5).
p152: (5) The tribunal shall consist of—
p152: (a) a person who holds or has held office as a judge of a superior
p152: court, who shall be the chairperson;
p152: (b) at least two persons who are qualified to be appointed as
p152: High Court judges; and
p152: (c) one other member who is qualified to assess the facts in
p152: respect of the particular ground for removal.
p152: (6) The tribunal shall investigate the matter expeditiously, report
p152: on the facts and make a binding recommendation to the President,
p152: who shall act in accordance with the recommendation within thirty
p152: days.
p152: (7) A person suspended under this Article is entitled to continue
p152: to receive one-half of the remuneration and benefits of the office while
p152: suspended.
p152: General functions and powers.
p152: 252. (1) Each commission, and each holder of an independent
p152: office—
p152: (a) may conduct investigations on its own initiative or on a
p152: complaint made by a member of the public;
p152: (b) has the powers necessary for conciliation, mediation and
p152: negotiation;
p152: (c) shall recruit its own staff; and
p152: (d) may perform any functions and exercise any powers
p152: prescribed by legislation, in addition to the functions and
p152: powers conferred by this Constitution.
p152: (2) A complaint to a commission or the holder of an independent
p152: office may be made by any person entitled to institute court
p152: proceedings under Article 22 (1) and (2).
p152: (3) The following commissions and independent offices have the
p152: power to issue a summons to a witness to assist for the purposes of its
p152: investigations—
p152: (a) the Kenya National Human Rights and Equality Commission;
p152: (b) the Judicial Service Commission;
p153: (c) the National Land Commission; and
p153: (d) the Auditor-General.
p153: Incorporation of commissions and independent offices.
p153: 253. Each commission and each independent office—
p153: (a) is a body corporate with perpetual succession and a seal; and
p153: (b) is capable of suing and being sued in its corporate name.
p153: Reporting by commissions and independent offices.
p153: 254. (1) As soon as practicable after the end of each financial
p153: year, each commission, and each holder of an independent office,
p153: shall submit a report to the President and to Parliament.
p153: (2) At any time, the President, the National Assembly or the
p153: Senate may require a commission or holder of an independent office to
p153: submit a report on a particular issue.
p153: (3) Every report required from a commission or holder of an
p153: independent office under this Article shall be published and publicised.
p153: CHAPTER SIXTEEN—AMENDMENT OF THIS CONSTITUTION
p153: Amendment of this Constitution.
p153: 255. (1) A proposed amendment to this Constitution shall be
p153: enacted in accordance with Article 256 or 257, and approved in
p153: accordance with clause (2) by a referendum, if the amendment relates
p153: to any of the following matters—
p153: (a) the supremacy of this Constitution;
p153: (b) the territory of Kenya;
p153: (c) the sovereignty of the people;
p153: (d) the national values and principles of governance referred to in
p153: Article 10 (2) (a) to (d);
p153: (e) the Bill of Rights;
p153: (f) the term of office of the President;
p153: (g) the independence of the Judiciary and the commissions and
p153: independent offices to which Chapter Fifteen applies;
p153: (h) the functions of Parliament;
p154: (i) the objects, principles and structure of devolved government;
p154: or
p154: (j) the provisions of this Chapter.
p154: (2) A proposed amendment shall be approved by a referendum
p154: under clause (1) if—
p154: (a) at least twenty per cent of the registered voters in each of at
p154: least half of the counties vote in the referendum; and
p154: (b) the amendment is supported by a simple majority of the
p154: citizens voting in the referendum.
p154: (3) An amendment to this Constitution that does not relate to a
p154: matter specified in clause (1) shall be enacted either—
p154: (a) by Parliament, in accordance with Article 256; or
p154: (b) by the people and Parliament, in accordance with Article 257.
p154: Amendment by parliamentary initiative.
p154: 256. (1) A Bill to amend this Constitution—
p154: (a) may be introduced in either House of Parliament;
p154: (b) may not address any other matter apart from consequential
p154: amendments to legislation arising from the Bill;
p154: (c) shall not be called for second reading in either House within
p154: ninety days after the first reading of the Bill in that House; and
p154: (d) shall have been passed by Parliament when each House of
p154: Parliament has passed the Bill, in both its second and third
p154: readings, by not less than two-thirds of all the members of
p154: that House.
p154: (2) Parliament shall publicise any Bill to amend this Constitution,
p154: and facilitate public discussion about the Bill.
p154: (3) After Parliament passes a Bill to amend this Constitution, the
p154: Speakers of the two Houses of Parliament shall jointly submit to the
p154: President—
p154: (a) the Bill, for assent and publication; and
p154: (b) a certificate that the Bill has been passed by Parliament in
p154: accordance with this Article.
p155: (4) Subject to clause (5), the President shall assent to the Bill and
p155: cause it to be published within thirty days after the Bill is enacted by
p155: Parliament.
p155: (5) If a Bill to amend this Constitution proposes an amendment
p155: relating to a matter specified in Article 255 (1)—
p155: (a) the President shall, before assenting to the Bill, request the
p155: Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission to
p155: conduct, within ninety days, a national referendum for
p155: approval of the Bill; and
p155: (b) within thirty days after the chairperson of the Independent
p155: Electoral and Boundaries Commission has certified to the
p155: President that the Bill has been approved in accordance with
p155: Article 255 (2), the President shall assent to the Bill and
p155: cause it to be published.
p155: Amendment by popular initiative.
p155: 257. (1) An amendment to this Constitution may be proposed by
p155: a popular initiative signed by at least one million registered voters.
p155: (2) A popular initiative for an amendment to this Constitution may
p155: be in the form of a general suggestion or a formulated draft Bill.
p155: (3) If a popular initiative is in the form of a general suggestion, the
p155: promoters of that popular initiative shall formulate it into a draft Bill.
p155: (4) The promoters of a popular initiative shall deliver the draft Bill
p155: and the supporting signatures to the Independent Electoral and
p155: Boundaries Commission, which shall verify that the initiative is
p155: supported by at least one million registered voters.
p155: (5) If the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission is
p155: satisfied that the initiative meets the requirements of this Article, the
p155: Commission shall submit the draft Bill to each county assembly for
p155: consideration within three months after the date it was submitted by
p155: the Commission.
p155: (6) If a county assembly approves the draft Bill within three
p155: months after the date it was submitted by the Commission, the
p155: speaker of the county assembly shall deliver a copy of the draft Bill
p155: jointly to the Speakers of the two Houses of Parliament, with a
p155: certificate that the county assembly has approved it.
p155: (7) If a draft Bill has been approved by a majority of the county
p155: assemblies, it shall be introduced in Parliament without delay.
p156: (8) A Bill under this Article is passed by Parliament if supported
p156: by a majority of the members of each House.
p156: (9) If Parliament passes the Bill, it shall be submitted to the
p156: President for assent in accordance with Articles 256 (4) and (5).
p156: (10) If either House of Parliament fails to pass the Bill, or the Bill
p156: relates to a matter specified in 255 (1), the proposed amendment shall
p156: be submitted to the people in a referendum.
p156: (11) Article 255 (2) applies, with any necessary modifications, to
p156: a referendum under clause (10).
p156: CHAPTER SEVENTEEN—GENERAL PROVISIONS
p156: Enforcement of this Constitution.
p156: 258. (1) Every person has the right to institute court proceedings,
p156: claiming that this Constitution has been contravened, or is threatened
p156: with contravention.
p156: (2) In addition to a person acting in their own interest, court
p156: proceedings under clause (1) may be instituted by—
p156: (a) a person acting on behalf of another person who cannot act in
p156: their own name;
p156: (b) a person acting as a member of, or in the interest of, a group
p156: or class of persons;
p156: (c) a person acting in the public interest; or
p156: (d) an association acting in the interest of one or more of its
p156: members.
p156: Construing this Constitution.
p156: 259. (1) This Constitution shall be interpreted in a manner that—
p156: (a) promotes its purposes, values and principles;
p156: (b) advances the rule of law, and the human rights and
p156: fundamental freedoms in the Bill of Rights;
p156: (c) permits the development of the law; and
p156: (d) contributes to good governance.
p156: (2) If there is a conflict between different language versions of
p156: this Constitution, the English language version prevails.
p157: (3) Every provision of this Constitution shall be construed
p157: according to the doctrine of interpretation that the law is always
p157: speaking and, therefore, among other things—
p157: (a) a function or power conferred by this Constitution on an office
p157: may be performed or exercised as occasion requires, by the
p157: person holding the office;
p157: (b) any reference in this Constitution to a State or other public
p157: office or officer, or a person holding such an office, includes a
p157: reference to the person acting in or otherwise performing the
p157: functions of the office at any particular time;
p157: (c) a reference in this Constitution to an office, State organ or
p157: locality named in this Constitution shall be read with any
p157: formal alteration necessary to make it applicable in the
p157: circumstances; and
p157: (d) a reference in this Constitution to an office, body or
p157: organisation is, if the office, body or organisation has ceased
p157: to exist, a reference to its successor or to the equivalent
p157: office, body or organisation.
p157: (4) In this Constitution, unless the context otherwise requires—
p157: (a) if a word or expression is defined in this Constitution, any
p157: grammatical variation or cognate expression of the word or
p157: expression has a corresponding meaning, read with the
p157: changes required by the context; and
p157: (b) the word “includes” means “includes, but is not limited to”.
p157: (5) In calculating time between two events for any purpose under
p157: this Constitution, if the time is expressed—
p157: (a) as days, the day on which the first event occurs shall be
p157: excluded, and the day by which the last event may occur
p157: shall be included;
p157: (b) as months, the time period ends at the beginning of the day in
p157: the relevant month—
p157: (i) that has the same number as the date on which the
p157: period began, if that month has a corresponding date;
p157: or
p157: (ii) that is the last day of that month, in any other case; or
p158: (c) as years, the period of time ends at the beginning of the date
p158: of the relevant year that corresponds to the date on which the
p158: period began.
p158: (6) If a period of time prescribed by this Constitution for any
p158: purpose is six days or less, Sundays and public holidays shall not
p158: count when calculating the time.
p158: (7) If, in any particular circumstances, the period of time
p158: prescribed by this Constitution ends on a Sunday or a public holiday,
p158: the period extends to the first subsequent day that is not a Sunday or
p158: public holiday.
p158: (8) If a particular time is not prescribed by this Constitution for
p158: performing a required act, the act shall be done without unreasonable
p158: delay, and as often as occasion arises.
p158: (9) If any person or State organ has authority under this
p158: Constitution to extend a period of time prescribed by this Constitution,
p158: the authority may be exercised either before or after the end of the
p158: period, unless a contrary intention is expressly specified in the
p158: provision conferring the authority.
p158: (10) Except to the extent that this Constitution provides
p158: otherwise, if a person has vacated an office established under this
p158: Constitution, the person may, if qualified, again be appointed, elected
p158: or otherwise selected to hold the office in accordance with this
p158: Constitution.
p158: (11) If a function or power conferred on a person under this
p158: Constitution is exercisable by the person only on the advice or
p158: recommendation, with the approval or consent of, or on consultation
p158: with, another person, the function may be performed or the power
p158: exercised only on that advice, recommendation, with that approval or
p158: consent, or after that consultation, except to the extent that this
p158: Constitution provides otherwise.
p158: Interpretation.
p158: 260. In this Constitution, unless the context requires otherwise—
p158: “adult” means an individual who has attained the age of eighteen
p158: years;
p158: “affirmative action” includes any measure designed to overcome
p158: or ameliorate an inequity or the systemic denial or infringement of a
p158: right or fundamental freedom;
p159: “child” means an individual who has not attained the age of
p159: eighteen years;
p159: “contravene” includes fail to comply with;
p159: “county legislation” means a law made by a county government
p159: or under authority conferred by a county Assembly;
p159: “disability” includes any physical, sensory, mental, psychological
p159: or other impairment, condition or illness that has, or is perceived by
p159: significant sectors of the community to have, a substantial or long-term
p159: effect on an individual’s ability to carry out ordinary day-to-day
p159: activities;
p159: “document” includes—
p159: (a) any publication, or any matter written, expressed, or inscribed
p159: on any substance by means of letters, figures or marks, or by
p159: more than one of those means, that is intended to be used or
p159: may be used for the purpose of recording that matter; and
p159: (b) electronic files;
p159: “effective date” means the date that this Constitution came into
p159: force;
p159: “fail” includes refuse;
p159: “financial year” means the period of twelve months ending on the
p159: thirtieth day of June or other day prescribed by national legislation, but
p159: the initial financial year of any entity is the period of time from its
p159: coming into existence until the immediately following thirtieth day of
p159: June, or other day prescribed by national legislation;
p159: “Gazette” means the Kenya Gazette published by authority of the
p159: national government, or a supplement to the Kenya Gazette;
p159: “guarantee” means any absolute or conditional promise,
p159: commitment or undertaking by the national government to partially or
p159: completely re-pay any loan to a county government or any person;
p159: “judicial officer” means a registrar, deputy registrar, magistrate,
p159: Kadhi or the presiding officer of a court established under Article 169
p159: (1) (d);
p159: “land” includes—
p159: (a) the surface of the earth and the subsurface rock;
p160: (b) any body of water on or under the surface;
p160: (c) marine waters in the territorial sea and exclusive economic
p160: zone;
p160: (d) natural resources completely contained on or under the
p160: surface; and
p160: (e) the air space above the surface;
p160: “legislation” includes—
p160: (a) an Act of Parliament, or a law made under authority conferred
p160: by an Act of Parliament; or
p160: (b) a law made by an assembly of a county government, or under
p160: authority conferred by such a law;
p160: “loan” includes any form of borrowing, lending or deferred
p160: payment in respect of which money from a public fund may be used, or
p160: is required to be used, for payment or repayment;
p160: “marginalised community” means—
p160: (a) a community that, because of its relatively small population or
p160: for any other reason, has been unable to fully participate in
p160: the integrated social and economic life of Kenya as a whole;
p160: (b) a traditional community that, out of a need or desire to
p160: preserve its unique culture and identity from assimilation, has
p160: remained outside the integrated social and economic life of
p160: Kenya as a whole;

p163: Consequential legislation.

p163: (3) The power of the National Assembly contemplated under
p163: clause (2), may be exercised—
p163: (a) only once in respect of any particular matter; and
p163: (b) only in exceptional circumstances to be certified by the
p163: Speaker of the National Assembly.

p163: consultation with the Commission for the Implementation of the

p163: Parliament, as soon as reasonably practicable, to enable Parliament to
p163: enact the legislation within the period specified.
p163: (5) If Parliament fails to enact any particular legislation within the
p163: specified time, any person may petition the High Court on the matter.
p163: (6) The High Court in determining a petition under clause (5) may
p163: —
p163: (a) make a declaratory order on the matter; and
p164: (b) transmit an order directing Parliament and the
p164: Attorney-General to take steps to ensure that the required
p164: legislation is enacted, within the period specified in the order,

p164: (7) If Parliament fails to enact legislation in accordance with an
p164: order under clause (6) (b), the Chief Justice shall advise the President

p164: specified in the Fifth Schedule beginning with the date of
p164: commencement of the term of the new Parliament.

p164: with clause (8), the provisions of clauses (1) to (8) shall apply afresh.

p191: PROMULGATION
p191: By His Excellency the Honorable Mwai Kibaki, President and
p191: Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces of the Republic of Kenya.
p191: WHEREAS the people of Kenya, in exercise of their sovereign right to
p191: replace the Constitution, ratified the proposed New Constitution of Kenya through a
p191: referendum held on the 4th August, 2010, in accordance with the provisions of section 47A
p191: of the Constitution of Kenya and Part V of the Constitution of Kenya Review Act, 2008;
p191: AND WHEREAS the Constitution of Kenya (Amendment) Act, 2008 and the
p191: Constitution of Kenya Review Acts of 1997 and 2008, as variously amended, provided a
p191: legal framework for the comprehensive review and replacement of the current Constitution
p191: by the people of Kenya, which ensured that the review process—
p191: (a) accommodated the diversity of the Kenyan people, including socio economic
p191: status, race, ethnicity, gender, religious faith, age, occupation, learning, persons
p191: with disabilities and the disadvantaged and was guided by respect for the
p191: universal principles of human rights, gender equity and democracy;
p191: (b) provided the people of Kenya an opportunity to actively, freely and meaningfully
p191: participate in generating and debating proposals to alter the Constitution;
p191: (c) resulted in a new Constitution which faithfully reflected the wishes of the people of
p191: Kenya;
p191: AND WHEREAS for the last two decades, the people of Kenya have yearned for a
p191: new Constitution which—
p191: (a) guarantees peace, national unity and integrity of the Republic of Kenya in order to
p191: safeguard the well-being of the people of Kenya;
p191: (b) establishes a free and democratic system of Government that ensures good
p191: governance, constitutionalism, the rule of law, human rights and gender equity;
p191: (c) recognizes and demarcates divisions of responsibility among the various state
p191: organs, including the executive, the legislature and the judiciary, so as to create
p191: checks and balances between them and to ensure accountability of the
p191: Government and its officers to the people of Kenya;
p192: [Subsidiary]
p192: (d) promotes the people’s participation in the governance of the country through
p192: democratic, free and fair elections and the devolution and exercise of power and
p192: further ensures the full participation of the people in the management of public
p192: affairs;
p192: (e) respects the pride of the people of Kenya in their ethnic, cultural and religious
p192: diversity and their determination to live in peace and unity as one indivisible
p192: sovereign nation;
p192: (f) ensures the provision of basic needs of all Kenyans through the establishment of
p192: an equitable framework for economic growth and equitable access to national
p192: resources;
p192: (g) strengthens national integration and unity and commits Kenyans to peaceful
p192: resolution of national issues through dialogue and consensus;
p192: NOW THEREFORE, in exercise of the powers conferred on me by section 47A (6) of
p192: the Constitution of Kenya and section 43A of the Constitution of Kenya Review Act, 2008, I,
p192: Mwai Kibaki, President and Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces of the Republic of
p192: Kenya, declare that the Constitution set out in the Schedule shall be the new Constitution of
p192: Kenya with effect from the 27th August, 2010.
